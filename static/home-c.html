Why we built this

In 2016 we left France for South Korea with our two cats, Lili and Lilou. We thought love would be enough. It wasn’t.

On the Asiana Airlines flight, having two cats in the cabin meant we couldn’t sit together. I watched my wife across the aisle while Lilou trembled in a carrier that was far too small for the tiny space under the seat. The engines roared. The cabin never stopped shaking. For a cat, that noise is a kind of hell — and Lilou fought to get out. She hurt her paw trying to escape the bag. I can still hear it.

I begged the flight attendants to let me take her to the lavatory so she could breathe, hide, calm down. They finally said yes. In that narrow bathroom, holding a frightened black-and-white cat against my chest, I promised myself we would never put a family through a flight like that unprepared again — not ours, and not anyone else’s.

Lili was there too, quiet in her own carrier, while we sat apart for hours of a journey that should have been a new beginning and instead felt like survival. We arrived in Korea exhausted, guilty, and certain of one thing: pet travel needs clear rules, real carrier sizes, and someone who has lived the hard version of the story.

Today Future, our Shih Tzu, is part of the family too. We built Paw Passport so you don’t have to learn the way we did — airline by airline, rule by rule, checked at the official source, so your dog or cat can cross the world without the panic we still remember.

Marc LEVRAT
