Pet travel rules for Spain: EU entry, travellers’ points and potentially dangerous dogs

Spain applies the EU pet-entry rules for dogs, cats and ferrets, and adds national points of its own: the EU certificate must be presented at least in Spanish, pets under 15 weeks are not admitted, and Spain still has national rules for potentially dangerous dogs. The EU framework in force since 22 April 2026 is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/705 (certificate models) and 2026/636 (listed countries). The Spanish ministry (MAPA) already cites those 2026 acts. Checked 11 November 2026. Always confirm with MAPA, the travellers’ point of entry and your airline before you travel.

Every entry rule below is labelled Official rule. Nothing on this page is labelled Field-Verified. USDA APHIS, on its Spain page last modified 2 September 2026, still refers to “EU 576/2013 Annex III” for rabies requirements, while describing the new EU certificates under EU 2026/131. Where that matters, it is flagged. Follow the current Commission, MAPA and APHIS certificate models.

Entry rules (MAPA)

Authority: MAPA — Viajar con la mascota. Perros, gatos, hurones. EU rules: European Commission — Bringing a pet into the EU from a non-EU country and the Commission list of territories and non-EU countries under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (Commission pages last confirmed 10 November 2026). For pets leaving the United States: USDA APHIS — Pet travel from the United States to Spain (dateModified 2 September 2026; checked 11 November 2026).

Dogs

  • Identification. Official rule An ISO transponder (microchip) meeting ISO 11784 and ISO 11785, or a tattoo applied before 3 July 2011 and still readable. The chip must be in place before the rabies vaccination. MAPA · APHIS Spain · Commission
  • Rabies vaccination. Official rule Vaccination at 12 weeks of age or older, after identification, with a vaccine that is valid at the time of travel. After a primary vaccination, wait at least 21 days before entry. APHIS treats a primary rabies vaccination given in the United States as valid for 1 year for EU purposes, even if a 3-year product was used; keep boosters without a gap. APHIS still cites “EU 576/2013 Annex III” for this requirement; MAPA and the Commission guide in force since 22 April 2026 state the same 12-week minimum and 21-day wait. MAPA · APHIS Spain
  • Rabies titer. Official rule Required when the pet comes from a territory or non-EU country that is not listed in Annex I or Annex II of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. The blood sample is taken at least 30 days after the primary vaccination (MAPA notes that the 30-day wait applies to a primary vaccination; a correctly maintained booster schedule does not restart that wait). The result must be at least 0.5 IU/ml from an authorised laboratory, and the animal may enter only after 90 days from the sample date. MAPA states that the minimum age of an animal arriving from a rabies-risk (unlisted) country is therefore 7 months. The United States, Canada, Mexico, Great Britain and Japan are listed (no titer). South Korea does not appear on the Commission list. If an EU-resident pet leaves the Union for an unlisted country and returns, the 90-day wait does not apply when the titer was completed, with a favourable result, before the pet left the EU, and is recorded in the EU pet passport. MAPA · Commission listing
  • EU animal health certificate. Official rule Dogs, cats and ferrets arriving from a non-EU country, other than Norway, Northern Ireland and the Annex I territories, and that do not already hold a valid EU pet passport, must travel with an EU animal health certificate signed by an official veterinarian of the third country, a written declaration, and a certified copy of the identification and vaccination data. MAPA requires the certificate to be presented at least in Spanish (castellano), using the EU models. MAPA links the certificate model valid from 22 April 2026 (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705). The Commission states that the certificate is valid for 10 days from issue until the checks at the travellers’ point of entry (extended by the length of a sea journey), and then for later travel to other EU countries for 6 months or until the rabies vaccination expires, whichever is earlier. Pet passports issued under the previous model remain valid; old-model passports issued before 1 January 2028 and animal health certificates issued before 1 October 2026 (usable until 31 March 2027) are deemed to comply (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, Art. 31; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705, Art. 6). MAPA · Commission
  • Number of pets and accompaniment. Official rule Non-commercial movement: 5 or fewer animals, not for sale or transfer of ownership, travelling with the owner or an authorised person (never alone). The owner may travel up to 5 days before or after the pet. The owner and the authorised person must be natural persons, not companies. When an authorised person travels, a written authorisation signed by the owner is attached to the identification document, in the format set out in Annex V, Part 1, of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705. Documented exceptions exist for competitions, exhibitions or sporting events for animals older than 6 months. MAPA
  • Minimum age. Official rule Spain does not grant exceptions and does not authorise entry of dogs, cats or ferrets younger than 15 weeks, and therefore not yet holding a valid rabies vaccination (minimum vaccination age 12 weeks, plus at least 21 days). MAPA
  • Tapeworm. Official rule Spain does not require Echinococcus treatment for entry into Spain. A dog travelling onwards to Ireland, Malta, Finland, Norway or Northern Ireland must be treated against E. multilocularis between 24 and 120 hours before arriving in that country. MAPA

Cats

  • Official rule The same identification, rabies, listed/unlisted titer, certificate or passport, accompaniment and age rules apply to cats (and ferrets) as to dogs. MAPA

These rules cover non-commercial movements. If the movement is commercial, including more than 5 animals without a documented exception, follow the commercial procedures, which this page does not set out. MAPA

Arriving from the USA, Canada, Japan, South Korea or the EU

From the United States

  • Official rule The United States is listed in Annex II of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 — no rabies titer. Commission listing · MAPA
  • Official rule A USDA-accredited veterinarian issues the EU non-commercial certificate and submits it through VEHCS. APHIS must ink-sign and emboss the certificate (electronic signature is accepted from the accredited veterinarian; original ink endorsement is required from APHIS). The endorsed original must travel with the pet. APHIS requires the endorsement to fall within 10 days of the pet’s arrival in the EU. It also states that the non-commercial certificate is valid for 30 days after the accredited veterinarian issues it, and that it may then be used for travel within the EU for up to 6 months from the day of entry, while the rabies vaccination remains valid. The owner or designated person must complete and sign the declaration. New non-commercial certificates took effect on 1 October 2026 (APHIS cites EU 2026/131). MAPA still requires the document to be presented at least in Spanish; use the bilingual EU model MAPA publishes for that purpose. If the pet already holds a valid EU pet passport, an export health certificate may not be needed — confirm on the APHIS page. APHIS Spain (dateModified 2 September 2026; checked 11 November 2026) · MAPA

From Canada

  • Official rule Canada is listed — no rabies titer. The pet still needs the ISO microchip, a valid rabies vaccination with the 21-day wait after a primary course, the EU animal health certificate and declaration (at least in Spanish), and entry through a travellers’ point of entry. Commission listing · MAPA

From Japan

  • Official rule Japan is listed — no rabies titer for entry to the EU. The same certificate, language, microchip and rabies-vaccination rules apply. Commission listing

From South Korea

  • Official rule The Republic of Korea (South Korea) does not appear in Annex I or Annex II on the Commission listing page last confirmed on 10 November 2026. Pets coming from Korea need a rabies antibody titration (sample at least 30 days after a primary vaccination, result at least 0.5 IU/ml) and a 90-day wait from the sample date, plus the EU animal health certificate and declaration, presented at least in Spanish. Commission listing · MAPA

From another EU country, or from an Annex I territory

  • Official rule From an EU member state, Norway, Northern Ireland, or an Annex I territory under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (Andorra, Switzerland, the Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Greenland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City State), the pet needs a microchip or a qualifying tattoo, a valid rabies vaccination recorded in an EU pet passport, and must be at least 15 weeks old. MAPA points to its instruction on introducing dogs, cats and ferrets from other member states. MAPA

Where you enter

  • Official rule Dogs, cats and ferrets arriving from a non-EU country must enter through an authorised travellers’ point of entry (Punto de Entrada de Viajeros, PEV). At the airport, port or road border, find the control point and declare the pet to the Resguardo Fiscal of the Guardia Civil or other authorised customs staff, and present the documents for documentary and identity checks, including a reading of the microchip. MAPA

The list linked from the ministry page, and opened for this guide, is headed as updated on 26 October 2026 and also cites Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131. If a pet is found not to comply, the measures listed on that document are redispatch, isolation or euthanasia. National rules in Spain

Potentially dangerous dogs

Official rule The national rules on potentially dangerous animals remain those of Law 50/1999 and Royal Decree 287/2002 (consolidated text, last update published 12 December 2007). Royal Decree 287/2002, Annex I, lists these breeds and their crosses as potentially dangerous dogs: Pit Bull Terrier; Staffordshire Bull Terrier; American Staffordshire Terrier; Rottweiler; Dogo Argentino; Fila Brasileiro; Tosa Inu; Akita Inu. Annex II adds dogs that have all or most of a set of morphological characteristics. Dogs that show a markedly aggressive character, or that have attacked people or other animals, are also treated as potentially dangerous once the competent authority has made that finding, after a veterinary report.

In public places, a potentially dangerous dog must wear a muzzle suited to its type, and must be led on a non-extendable chain or leash less than 2 metres long. One person may not walk more than one such dog. The person in control must carry the administrative licence and the municipal registration certificate. The licence requires, among other conditions, civil-liability insurance with cover of not less than €120,000. All potentially dangerous dogs of the canine species must be identified by microchip. Royal Decree 287/2002 (consolidated) · Law 50/1999

Autonomous communities and municipalities may add their own conditions. This page does not invent those local rules, and it does not restate a general national leash rule for dogs that are not potentially dangerous.

Ceuta and Melilla

Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish autonomous cities. They are listed separately on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list dated 15 April 2026 (checked 11 November 2026). A dog that has been in Ceuta or Melilla in the past 6 months follows the CDC high-risk path when entering the United States, even if it then travels via mainland Spain. CDC — high-risk countries

Airlines and ferry operators set their own pet-carriage rules, in addition to the official entry rules. APHIS Spain. For IATA-style hard crates used in the hold, see the existing IATA hard crates for cargo product page.

Return to the United States

Official rule CDC: every dog entering the United States needs a CDC Dog Import Form receipt, must be 6 months or older, must have a microchip, and must appear healthy. Mainland Spain is not on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list dated 15 April 2026 (checked 11 November 2026), provided the dog has not been in a high-risk country — including Ceuta or Melilla — in the past 6 months. Cats: appear healthy at federal level; state and airline rules still apply. An EU pet passport does not replace the CDC Dog Import Form for dogs. Taxi and private hire (VTC)

Uber Pet (Spain)

Official rule Uber publishes a Spain rider page for Uber Pet. Where the option appears in the app, you can request a pet-friendly ride. Uber says you can travel with up to 3 passengers and 1 pet, and that you can bring one pet per trip, such as a dog or a cat; availability may vary by location. Use a lead, harness or carrier for a dog or a cat; other pets should travel in a carrier. Uber says journeys may include an additional fee compared with other ride options, and a cleaning fee may apply if the animal leaves waste, excessive hair or causes damage. The rider page does not publish a fixed extra amount — check the upfront price in the app. You do not need to select Uber Pet when travelling with an assistance animal; you can request any ride option. Uber Pet (Spain) Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule Royal Decree 409/2025 of 27 May (in force from 17 June 2025) guarantees the assistance-dog unit — the user and the dog — the right of access, circulation and stay in public or private transport of public use, including collective transport. The dog does not count as a seat for capacity or for any maximum number of dogs, and no ticket or extra payment may be charged for the dog. For air transport, admission depends on prior notification to the airline, its agent or the tour operator, under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2006. The decree does not create a duty for an ordinary taxi or private-hire driver to accept a pet dog or cat. BOE — Royal Decree 409/2025 In force from 17 June 2025; checked 14 Nov 2026

Bus and metro (EMT Madrid, Metro de Madrid and TMB Barcelona)

Official rule The EMT Madrid Travellers’ Regulations, approved by Decree 206/2000 and still published by EMT (the copy on the operator site shows an update date of 29 February 2008; fetched 14 November 2026), require travellers not to travel with animals, except guide dogs accompanying a person who is blind, or small domestic animals carried by their owners in suitable containers that do not cause nuisance by smell or noise, or otherwise reduce other travellers’ comfort. People who are blind or have a serious visual impairment may board with a guide dog on the terms of Law 23/1998 of the Community of Madrid. EMT Madrid — regulations · EMT Travellers’ Regulations (PDF) Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule For Metro de Madrid, Decree 73/2016 (Official Gazette of the Community of Madrid, 6 July 2016) allows assistance dogs, including assistance dogs in training, on the terms of Law 2/2015 of the Community of Madrid. Other dogs may board if they are correctly identified with a microchip, on the hours, lines or sections set by the Regional Transport Consortium in a resolution published in that Official Gazette, and if these conditions are met: one dog per traveller; a muzzle, and a lead no longer than 50 cm, from the station entrance until you leave on the street; travel in the last car of the train; the dog may not occupy a seat; lifts may be used if they are not blocked and occupancy allows it; escalators and moving walkways may not be used. The operator may limit access in crowds or for safety. Small domestic animals may travel in a suitable container from which they cannot escape, if they are not dangerous or a nuisance by shape, size, noise or smell. Check the current Consortium hours before you travel. BOCM, 6 July 2016 — Decreto 73/2016 (PDF) Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule TMB (Barcelona metro and bus), official passenger-conduct page: guide dogs, assistance dogs identified with a badge and a blue cape, animals accompanying security staff, and pets carried in a suitable travel basket prepared so that they cannot soil or inconvenience other users, may travel without time limits on both metro and bus. In addition, on the metro, dogs that are electronically identified with a chip and registered in the municipal census may board if they meet these conditions: on weekdays between 11 September and 24 June, from 5:00 to 7:00, from 9:00 to 17:00 and from 19:30 until closing; at weekends and on public holidays, and every day from 24 June to 11 September, there is no time limit; the operator may restrict admission when the system is crowded. Only one dog per person; from the metro entrance until you leave on the street the dog must wear a muzzle and be held on a non-extendable lead at a distance of less than 50 cm; dogs may use lifts and stone stairs but not escalators, and they may not occupy a seat. A person under 18 may not handle a dog classed as potentially dangerous. TMB — rules and conduct Checked 14 Nov 2026

Train (Renfe)

Official rule Renfe’s official pets page (fetched 14 November 2026): on AVE, AVE between Spain and France, and Long Distance, small companion animals (dogs, cats, ferrets, hamsters, guinea pigs, rabbits and non-poultry birds) up to 10 kg may travel in a carrier of at most 60 × 35 × 35 cm, one pet per person, with a pet ticket and without occupying a seat. The pet ticket is free with a Prémium ticket (not available on AVE between Spain and France); with a Básico or Elige ticket it costs €10. On Avlo the same size and weight rules apply and the pet ticket is €10. On Regional and Avant the pet ticket costs 25% of the general fare. On metric-gauge Regional services, dogs, cats and birds travel free, with no weight limit, on a lead (dogs must wear a muzzle), one per person, and a carrier is not required. On Cercanías, dogs, cats and birds travel free, with no weight limit, one per person, on a lead and muzzle; a carrier is not required. Pets are not admitted on combined train+bus tickets. Renfe: travelling with pets Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule Renfe also offers travel for a dog of up to 40 kg without a carrier on AVE, Avlo, Long Distance, Avant and Regional trains (except connecting trains and trains with a programmed alternative transport plan), when the train is marked with a paw symbol at purchase. Potentially dangerous breeds, dogs under 1 year and bitches in heat are excluded. One large dog per person and a maximum of two large dogs per train, in a designated car. On AVE, Avlo and Long Distance the dog occupies the adjoining inward window seat; a muzzle and a non-extendable lead of at most 1.5 m are required in the station and when boarding or leaving; during the journey the muzzle is not required, but the dog must stay on its assigned place. On Avant and Regional the dog may not travel on the seat. The “Perros hasta 40 kg” extra costs €40 on AVE (Spanish section of AVE to France included), Avlo and Long Distance, and is limited to Elige Estándar on AVE and Long Distance; on Avant and Regional the extra is the equivalent of a general-fare ticket. It can be bought up to 24 hours before departure and cannot be changed. You must present an up-to-date vaccination card (signed and stamped by a veterinarian) or a European pet passport, and bring your own cleaning kit. Flights — Iberia, Vueling and Spain entry

Airline cabin and hold rules are separate from Spain’s entry rules (chip, rabies, listed or unlisted titre, EU certificate or passport, a designated travellers’ point of entry, and at least Spanish on the certificate). A pet accepted by the airline can still be refused at the border.

Iberia

Official rule Iberia Group (Iberia, Iberia Regional / Air Nostrum and Iberia Express): dogs, cats, turtles and birds (except poultry and birds of prey) may travel in the cabin if the animal and carrier together do not exceed 8 kg. The carrier may be at most 45 cm long, 35 cm wide and 25 cm high, and the three dimensions together must not exceed 105 cm; it must be sturdy, ventilated (at least 16% of the four sides) and waterproof, and must stay under the seat. On flights operated by Air Nostrum the carrier may not be rigid. Up to three animals of the same species may share one container if they are small enough; the published price is per carrier. The animal must stay in the carrier for the whole flight and may not use an emergency-exit row. Cabin supplements on the official table (subject to change) include, for a one-way journey in Spain except the Canary Islands, EUR 40 / USD 50 / GBP 35 if booked online and EUR 44 / USD 55 / GBP 40 at the airport; other zones and connecting itineraries are listed on the same table. In the hold, only dogs and cats are accepted, excluding brachycephalic animals and dogs of the dangerous-breed list published on the page; the maximum weight is 45 kg including the carrier; prior authorisation is required, and requests are accepted no later than 48 hours before departure. Hold connections must be between 90 minutes and 4 hours. Mustelids (including ferrets) are not accepted as pets. Pets are not accepted in the cabin on flights to London Heathrow, Manchester or Edinburgh, or in the cabin on arrival or departure at London Gatwick, and they are not accepted in the hold to or from any UK airport. Iberia: travelling with pets Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule Iberia carries recognised assistance dogs in the cabin free of charge: guide dogs, service dogs, hearing (signal) dogs, medical-alert dogs, dogs for people with autism-spectrum disorders, fully trained rescue dogs travelling with a Spanish law-enforcement or rescue team, and dogs in training accompanied by their trainer with an original certificate from the organisation. Therapy or emotional-support dogs are not treated as assistance dogs and follow the ordinary pet rules. You should notify Iberia when you book, or preferably at least 48 hours before departure, and present an official user-and-dog card or badge or, if you cannot, a certificate from an IGDF or ADI member school, or an ONCE guide-dog user credential. The dog stays at your feet, on a lead, collar or harness, and may not use an emergency-exit row. Iberia: assistance dogs Checked 14 Nov 2026

Vueling

Official rule Vueling accepts pets only in the cabin, not in the hold: dogs, cats, non-raptor birds and turtles, in an approved non-rigid carrier of at most 45 × 39 × 21 cm and 10 kg including the animal. One pet per person, and a maximum of five pets per flight (two on flights operated by Iberia). Up to two dogs or cats may share a carrier if they are of the same species and/or litter and stay within the size and weight limits. The carrier must go under the seat in front and must not be opened until you have left the aircraft. The service is not available on flights to or from the United Kingdom or Iceland. It is booked with a Vueling Fly Light fare or an Iberia Basic fare; if you already have a booking, add the pet through the customer-service centre. The operator pages checked on 14 November 2026 do not publish a single fixed euro amount — check the price when you book. Vueling: travelling with pets · Vueling Help: pets Checked 14 Nov 2026

Parks and dog spaces

Official rule The City of Madrid page “Los perros en los parques de Madrid” (fetched 14 November 2026), applying the municipal ordinance on the keeping and protection of animals, says dogs may go through parks on a lead, except in the official off-lead time bands: 19:00 to 10:00 in the winter timetable and 20:00 to 10:00 in the summer timetable. When off the lead, the person in charge must keep the dog in sight and close enough to intervene. Dogs may not enter children’s or older-people’s areas, or any area where entry is expressly forbidden, and they may not enter three parks: the historic garden of El Capricho de la Alameda de Osuna, the Quinta de Torre Arias and the Jardines de Sabatini. Dogs classed as potentially dangerous must always be on a lead and muzzled in public places. The city also provides fenced dog areas in all 21 districts. Bathing in ornamental fountains or ponds, and drinking directly from public taps, is forbidden. Ayuntamiento de Madrid: dogs in parks Checked 14 Nov 2026

In urban parks, unless they are dog-recreation zones, dogs must be on a lead. They may not enter some historic or artistic gardens where a ban is posted at the entrance, children’s areas, or beaches in the bathing season, except a delimited zone on Platja de Llevant. Dogs classed as potentially dangerous must always be on a lead and muzzled. The page states that the city has more than 200 spaces for dogs, including 116 dog areas (recreation areas over 400 m² and other areas). Grooming

Spanish salons set their own rules on vaccines and documents. No official national grooming tariff was published on a government or veterinary-council page opened for this guide — call ahead and do not assume a US or Korean rabies certificate will be accepted. Also see the Grooming directory.

K9 and assistance dogs

Official rule Royal Decree 409/2025 of 27 May (in force from 17 June 2025) regulates assistance dogs during training, work and retirement. It classifies, at a minimum, guide dogs, sound-alert dogs, service dogs, medical-alert dogs and dogs for people with autism-spectrum disorders. The user-and-dog unit has a right of access, circulation and stay in public or private places of public use — including shops, restaurants, tourist accommodation, workplaces and collective transport — without an extra charge, and the dog does not count as a seat or toward a dog quota. For air travel, the airline must be notified in advance, under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2006. Recognition of the unit by one autonomous community is valid throughout Spain. Assistance dogs recognised in another country, in the same categories, that accompany a person who does not reside in Spain, have the same access rights. Access may be limited if the dog is clearly in an inadequate hygienic condition, or if there is an imminent and serious risk; operating theatres and food-preparation areas remain restricted. BOE — Royal Decree 409/2025 In force from 17 June 2025; checked 14 Nov 2026

The Fundación ONCE del Perro Guía restates the national right of access for guide-dog users and points to Royal Decree 409/2025 and Royal Decree 193/2023. Guide dogs in training may use public or collective private spaces and transport on the same terms when they are with an accredited trainer or socialisation agent and are visibly identified as a dog in training. Fundación ONCE del Perro Guía — right of access Checked 14 Nov 2026

Renfe carries guide and assistance dogs free of charge, without weight or carriage limits, including dogs in training under Royal Decree 409/2025. TMB admits guide dogs and assistance dogs (badge and blue cape) without time limits. Iberia carries recognised assistance dogs, and fully trained rescue dogs travelling with a Spanish law-enforcement or rescue team, free in the cabin. On Uber, you do not need to select Uber Pet when you travel with an assistance animal. Details and links are in the train, bus, flights and taxi sections above.

The European Commission guide to bringing a pet into the EU from a non-EU country does not describe a separate health-rule waiver for working or police dogs. A military or detection dog that is not travelling as a recognised assistance dog still needs the ordinary entry rules (microchip, rabies vaccination, titre if the country is unlisted, EU certificate or passport, and a designated travellers’ point of entry). Ask the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food (MAPA) if you need a special arrangement for an official working dog. See also the K9 guide.

FAQ

Do I need a rabies titer for Spain from the United States, Canada or Japan?

Official rule No. Those countries are listed under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. South Korea is not listed, so a titer and a 90-day wait apply from Korea.

Which certificate model should I use in 2026, and in which language?

Official rule The current EU non-commercial model is the Annex III certificate under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705, valid from 22 April 2026. APHIS required the new non-commercial form from 1 October 2026. MAPA requires the certificate to be presented at least in Spanish. Use the bilingual model published by MAPA.

Can I fly into any of the 43 Aena passenger airports with a pet from the United States?

No. From a third country the pet must enter through an authorised travellers’ point of entry. Several busy Aena airports are not on the 26 October 2026 MAPA list as airport entry points. Check that list, and your airline, before you book.

Are Ceuta and Melilla treated like mainland Spain for a later return to the United States?

Not by CDC. Both are on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list. A dog that has been in either city in the past 6 months follows the high-risk US entry path.
