Pet travel rules for Australia: DAFF import, Melbourne arrival and Mickleham quarantine

Australia only accepts cats and dogs from approved countries, in three groups. For most origins, including the United States (except Hawaii and Guam), you should plan on about six to seven months, a BICON import permit, a rabies titre and a stay at the Mickleham post-entry quarantine facility after a cargo arrival at Melbourne. Checked against official DAFF pages (pack of 12 November 2026) and re-checked assistance-dog, Victorian public-transport, taxi and airline pages on 14 November 2026. Always confirm with the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF) and your airline before you travel.

Every entry rule below is labelled Official rule. Nothing here is legal or veterinary advice.

Entry rules (DAFF)

Authority: DAFF — bringing cats and dogs to Australia · How to import · Group 3 step-by-step guide for dogs. Checked 12 Nov 2026

  • Official rule Approved countries are in three groups. Group 2 (recognised by DAFF as rabies-free) includes Japan, Hawaii, Guam, Iceland and Singapore. Group 3 (rabies present but well controlled) includes the United States except Hawaii and Guam, Canada, the United Kingdom, listed EU member states, and South Korea. Animals from a non-approved origin must first reside 180 days in an approved country. A valid biosecurity import permit is required in most cases, applied for through BICON.
  • Official rule The microchip must be ISO-compatible (10 or 15 digits). Numbers starting with 999, and 9-digit AVID chips, are not accepted. Pet passports are not accepted as the import document.
  • Official rule Group 3 rabies pathway: vaccination from 84 days of age; an RNATT (FAVN or RFFIT) of at least 0.5 IU/ml; the sample taken between 12 months and 180 days before export; and a 180-day wait from the date the sample arrives at the laboratory (this is a residency period, not quarantine). If vaccination or the RNATT lapses, the 180-day wait restarts.
  • Official rule Arrival for post-entry quarantine (PEQ) imports is at Melbourne International Airport only, as manifested cargo in an IATA crate (assistance dogs may be exempt from the cabin rule — see the K9 section). There is no domestic transfer into Melbourne for that arrival. A biosecurity officer takes the animal to Mickleham PEQ, 135 Donnybrook Road, Mickleham VIC 3064. Home quarantine is not allowed. Group 3 quarantine is 30 days, or 10 days with the official identity check (or Australian-origin export certification). Group 2 dogs stay at least 10 days. A stay can be extended up to 180 days if there is a biosecurity issue.

Published DAFF fee tables and the full parasite, test and breed-ban lists are on the official guides above. See also APHIS: US to Australia and the route guide USA to Australia.

Return to the United States

Official rule Dogs entering the United States need the CDC Dog Import Form and must meet the age, microchip and health rules. Australia is not named on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list dated 15 April 2026. Confirm on the current CDC pages before you fly. CDC: bringing a dog into the United States · CDC high-risk countries

Pet-friendly taxi

There is no single official national taxi rule for ordinary pet dogs and cats. Acceptance of an ordinary pet is set by the operator or the state or territory — ask before you book.

Official rule In New South Wales, under the Point to Point Transport (Taxis and Hire Vehicles) Regulation 2017, the driver of a taxi or hire vehicle (including rideshare) must not refuse to carry a passenger with an assistance animal or an assistance animal in training. Allergy, or a religious or cultural objection, is not an acceptable reason to refuse. The official fact sheet states that the penalty notice for refusal is $3,000 and that the maximum penalty for the offence is $5,500. A fee cannot be charged for hair or fur shedding by an assistance animal. That fact sheet does not set out a duty to accept an ordinary pet. NSW Point to Point: assistance animals Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule Safe Transport Victoria (page updated 2 October 2025) states that assistance animals, including guide dogs, are allowed in all taxis and rideshare vehicles in Victoria, and that it is an offence to refuse service to a passenger with their assistance animal. Passengers who are refused can complain to Safe Transport Victoria (1800 638 802), to the Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission, or to the booking provider. The official page does not publish a dollar penalty amount. Safe Transport Victoria: helping passengers with assistance animals Checked 14 Nov 2026

Train and public transport (Victoria)

Official rule Public Transport Victoria: guide dogs and assistance animals travel free on all Victorian public transport services, including seeing-eye dogs, hearing dogs, guide dogs in training, and assistance animals using an assistance animal pass or interstate accreditation. PTV: animals on public transport Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule On metropolitan trams and buses (including rail-replacement buses), only small animals in a suitable animal container are allowed. On metropolitan trains, small animals may travel in a suitable container, and dogs may also travel on a lead and wearing a muzzle; the dog must be kept under control, must not sit on seats or block doors or aisles, and you should avoid weekdays between 7:00 and 9:00 and between 16:00 and 18:00. On V/Line trains, small animals including cats and dogs may travel in a suitable container that meets V/Line’s guidelines. Animals are not allowed on V/Line coaches, including rail-replacement buses, unless they are a guide dog or an accredited assistance animal.

No official national (all-states) public-transport pet rule was opened in this pass. Check the operator in the state or territory you will use.

Flights

Official rule For PEQ imports, DAFF requires arrival at Melbourne as manifested cargo; ordinary pets are not arriving in the cabin on that pathway (an assistance-dog exception is described on the DAFF assistance-dog page). Checked 12–14 Nov 2026

Official rule Qantas: animals and pets travelling on a Qantas flight travel in a special area in the hold of a passenger aircraft, where the temperature and noise are similar to those in the cabin. Book the pet’s flight before your own, because there are restrictions on which flights accept animals. For Australian domestic travel, bookings are made with Qantas Freight. For international travel, Qantas strongly recommends using a pet travel specialist. Guide, hearing and assistance dogs may travel in the passenger cabin; use the official service-dogs page for the application rules. The official pets page opened on 14 November 2026 does not publish a single fare in dollars. Qantas: travelling with pets · Qantas: service dogs Checked 14 Nov 2026

Official rule Virgin Australia Pets in Cabin (terms and conditions as at 13 October 2025): the service is available on selected domestic flights operated by Virgin Australia; it is not available on flights operated by third parties on behalf of Virgin Australia or on codeshare and other partner airlines. A pet is a small domesticated dog or cat, at least 8 weeks old, that is not a prohibited breed (including crossbreeds) listed on the airline’s website. The carrier must fit completely under the seat, must not exceed 44 cm (length) × 28 cm (width) × 28 cm (height), must be soft-sided, and the combined weight of the pet and the carrier must not exceed 8 kg. One pet per carrier and one carrier per guest. Add the pet by contacting the Guest Contact Centre at least 72 hours before departure. Check in with the pet no later than 75 minutes before the scheduled departure time. The fee is the amount published on the website at the time of booking (per pet, one-way, per sector); the terms state that published fees can change, so this guide does not restate a dollar amount. Assistance animals are a separate product; see the official assistance-animal page. Virgin Australia: Pets in Cabin terms · Virgin Australia: assistance animals Checked 14 Nov 2026

K9 and assistance dogs

Official rule DAFF’s official page “Travelling to Australia with an assistance dog” (fetched 14 November 2026) states that all assistance dogs coming to Australia must meet import conditions, including Australian dogs returning to Australia. Eligible assistance dogs are defined in the Biosecurity Regulation 2016: dogs professionally trained to assist a person with disability, with medical evidence of need. You can only import from an approved country. From most approved countries an import permit is required, applied for in BICON under “Assistance Dog”. DAFF says the process may take around six months or more. Notify the department of your arrival at least three working days ahead. Assistance dogs must travel to Australia by aircraft; you cannot arrive on an international cruise ship. From New Zealand and Norfolk Island, assistance dogs do not need an import permit and do not need post-entry quarantine; you may request reasonable adjustments such as in-cabin travel or a fee waiver when you notify the regional office. DAFF: travelling to Australia with an assistance dog Checked 14 Nov 2026

DAFF is seeking feedback on the import pathway for assistance animals (cats and dogs) until 27 November 2026. In line with the Disability Discrimination Act 1992, DAFF will consider reasonable adjustments as part of the permit process, provided biosecurity risk is managed and standard import conditions are still met. On Victorian public transport, guide dogs and assistance animals travel free — see the train section. See also the K9 guide.

FAQ

Can my dog arrive in the cabin in Melbourne as an ordinary pet?

Official rule For a PEQ import, no: DAFF requires manifested cargo in an IATA crate at Melbourne. Eligible assistance dogs may be exempt from that cabin rule — use the official assistance-dog pathway. Checked 12–14 Nov 2026

Can I take a dog on a Melbourne tram without a carrier?

Official rule Not as an ordinary pet: on metropolitan trams and buses, only small animals in a suitable container are allowed. Guide dogs and assistance animals travel free on all Victorian public transport services. Checked 14 Nov 2026
