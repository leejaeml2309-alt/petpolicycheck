Travelling with a dog or cat between the USA and Canada

Personal pets moving between the United States and Canada usually need only a valid rabies certificate to enter Canada, and the free CDC Dog Import Form (plus age and microchip rules) to return to the United States. Always confirm with CFIA and CDC before you travel.

Quick answer

  • Official rule Dogs older than 8 months and cats entering Canada as personal pets: a current rabies vaccination certificate from a licensed vet (English or French, identifying the animal) is enough. No USDA APHIS health-certificate endorsement is required for these cases. Source Checked Nov 9, 2026
  • Official rule Dogs 3–8 months travelling with their owner: rabies certificate if 3 months or older. Dogs under 3 months: no rabies vaccine required, but carry proof of age. Source Checked Nov 9, 2026
  • Official rule Unaccompanied dogs under 8 months: health certificate issued by a licensed US vet within 72 hours of arrival in Canada; APHIS endorsement is not required for that certificate. Source Checked Nov 9, 2026
  • Official rule Return to the US (dogs): Canada is not on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list. Every dog needs the free CDC Dog Import Form receipt, must be 6 months or older, microchipped, and look healthy. The same receipt can be reused for 6 months for US↔Canada trips if the dog visits no other country. Source Checked Nov 9, 2026
  • Official rule Cats returning to the US: no federal rabies proof or USDA health certificate required; the cat must appear healthy. State and airline rules still apply. Source Checked Nov 9, 2026

Rules change. Confirm on the official pages linked in each row before you book. Last checked Nov 9, 2026.

Checklist — USA → Canada

  • Rabies certificate (dogs ≥3 mo / cats ≥3 mo) — Official rule
  • Proof of age if dog or cat under 3 months
  • Health certificate ≤72 h if unaccompanied dog under 8 months
  • Airline pet booking + any airline health form
  • No APHIS VEHCS endorsement needed for typical personal adult dogs/cats (confirm your case on APHIS Canada page)

FAQ

Do I need USDA APHIS endorsement to take my adult dog to Canada?

Usually no. APHIS states pet dogs older than 8 months and cats do not require a health certificate for Canada — only proof of rabies vaccination. Confirm your exact case on the APHIS Canada page. Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Is Canada high-risk for dog rabies under CDC rules?

No. Canada is not on the CDC high-risk list, so dogs that stayed only in low-risk countries need the CDC Dog Import Form, age, microchip and a healthy look — not an extra rabies titer for CDC. Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Can the same CDC Dog Import Form receipt be reused?

Yes for frequent US↔Canada travel for up to 6 months, unless the dog visits a different country or a high-risk country. Checked Nov 9, 2026.
