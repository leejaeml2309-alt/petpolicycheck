Snub-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds: airline rules

Official cabin and hold/cargo rules for short-nosed dogs and cats on the 12 airlines already on PetPolicyCheck. Every row is an Official rule checked Nov 9, 2026.

How to read this table

Cabin = pet travelling with you under the seat. Hold / cargo = baggage compartment on your flight or airline cargo. Named breed lists are linked when the official page publishes them. This is not veterinary advice — always confirm with the airline before you book. 10 of 12 rows are marked complete; Air France (ordinary-pet hold) and British Airways (partner case-by-case) still have gaps.

What it means for owners

  • Cabin is often the only option when hold or cargo refuses snub-nosed breeds (Air Canada, Alaska/Hawaiian, American, Delta Cargo, Korean Air, Lufthansa).
  • Named lists differ by airline. Southwest and United state no breed restriction / no breed limitations for cabin pets.
  • Mixes usually count — Korean Air explicitly includes mixes of snub-nosed with non-snub-nosed breeds.
  • Heat and cold limits can still block a booking even when the breed is allowed.

These points only restate published airline rules. They are not veterinary advice.

FAQ

Can a French Bulldog fly in the cabin?

Often yes if the dog and carrier fit under the seat and the airline accepts cabin pets on that route. United and Southwest state no breed limitation / no breed restriction for cabin. Korean Air allows cabin when cabin conditions are met. Reserve early.

Why do airlines refuse snub-nosed breeds in the hold?

Airlines that publish a reason cite higher risk of heat stroke and breathing problems under stress or heat. That is the airline’s stated rationale, not a diagnosis from PetPolicyCheck.

Which airlines publish a named brachycephalic list?

American (checked + PetEmbark), Lufthansa, Alaska Air Cargo, Delta Cargo, and Korean Air (Korean passenger guide). Others either have no public hold option, say no breed restriction, or do not name breeds.

Does United or Southwest ban Pugs or Bulldogs?

United: no weight or breed limitations for in-cabin pets. Southwest: no breed restriction stated. Both are cabin-only for the public.

What about Persian or Himalayan cats?

Where lists include cats (Alaska Air Cargo, American, Lufthansa, Delta Cargo, Korean Air), those breeds are named. Air Canada’s brachy text refers to dogs.
