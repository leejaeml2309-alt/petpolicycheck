Pet-friendly hotels in Atlanta: chain and luxury hotel pet fees, weight limits and local tips

Fees, weight limits and the number of pets differ by brand and by hotel in Atlanta. Below: brand-wide chain rules, 5 Atlanta hotels whose own official pages publish a pet policy, and ATL airport pet relief areas.

Policies change: confirm with the hotel. Pet fees, weight limits and the number of pets are set by each brand and often by each hotel, and can change without notice. We read every chain’s official page on Nov 2, 2026. Call or message the hotel to confirm before you book, and declare your pet at check-in.

Quick answer

  • No pet fee: Kimpton charges no pet fee and sets no size, weight or number limit; Motel 6 lets up to 2 dogs or cats stay free (150 lb combined). Source
  • Budget chains: Red Roof: first pet free, second $15 per night, 80 lb per pet. La Quinta: optional fee up to $25 per night, max $75 per stay, 2 pets. Source
  • Higher fees: Drury charges a daily pet fee of at least $50 (varies by hotel), 2 pets, 80 lb combined. Source
  • Hilton, Aloft, Four Seasons, Loews: Their official pages give no single brand-wide pet fee or weight limit (Loews allows 2 pets): each hotel sets its own rules, so check the hotel page. Source
  • Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL): Indoor relief areas near T7, A10, B33, C19, D Centerpoint, E14 and F7; outdoor Poochie Park outside doors W1-W2 (Domestic South) and outside door A1 (International arrivals). Source

Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) pet relief areas

ATL says its Service Animal Relief Areas are open to all passenger pets; each has pet supplies, turf, a drinking fountain, waste bags and a sink. Official source

  • Indoor relief areas, always open, near: T7, A10, B33, C19, D Centerpoint, E14 and F7.
  • Outdoor: Poochie Park in Ground Transportation on Domestic Terminal South, outside doors W1 and W2; and outside door A1 on the International Terminal arrivals level.

Before you book

  • Filter for pet-friendly, then open the hotel’s own policy: fee per night or per stay, weight limit, number of pets, and whether cats are accepted.
  • Ask whether your pet may stay alone in the room: Motel 6 and Drury say pets should not be left alone.
  • Service animals are not pets. Ask the hotel how it handles service animals and emotional support animals rather than assuming a pet fee or limit applies.
