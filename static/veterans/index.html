Service dog or emotional support animal? What changed on US flights in 2021

Official rule (US DOT, Air Carrier Access Act, 14 CFR Part 382)

  • DOT published its final rule Traveling by Air with Service Animals on December 10, 2020 (announced December 2, 2020). It took effect on January 11, 2021.
  • A service animal is now a dog, of any breed or type, that is individually trained to do work or perform tasks for a person with a disability, including a psychiatric disability.
  • Emotional support animals, comfort animals, companionship animals and service animals in training are not service animals. Airlines may treat an emotional support animal as a pet. Some airlines may still choose to carry them free of charge, but they don't have to.
  • Psychiatric service dogs must be treated the same as other service dogs. The old extra steps for them, such as a letter from a mental health professional and checking in an hour early, were removed.
  • The only documents an airline may require are DOT's forms: the DOT Service Animal Air Transportation Form, and for flights of 8 hours or more, the relief attestation. Each airline has its own way to submit them. See the US air travel panel above for timing.

What this means for veterans with PTSD

  • A psychiatric service dog trained to perform specific tasks for your PTSD is a service animal under DOT rules. It flies in the cabin with no pet fee, subject to the same rules as any other service dog.
  • A dog that helps by its presence alone, through comfort, companionship or emotional support, is an emotional support animal. The airline may apply its pet policy, which can mean pet fees, carrier size limits and caps on how many animals it carries.
  • Under DOT's definition, the dog's training is what matters. A diagnosis or a letter alone does not make a dog a service animal. DOT does not require training by a specific school: owner-trained dogs can qualify if they meet the definition.
  • DOT's form carries a federal false-statement warning (18 U.S.C. § 1001). Only attest to training your dog actually has.

Sources: DOT — Service Animals · DOT — Final Rule announcement (Dec 2, 2020) · DOT — Final Rule page · Federal Register, 85 FR 79742 Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Help with service dog costs: VA (US) and Veterans Affairs Canada

Each point separates what the program is from who qualifies. Eligibility is decided by VA or VAC, not by PetPolicyCheck.

United States — Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)

1. Service Dog Veterinary Health Benefit (38 CFR 17.148)

  • What it is: VA does not provide service dogs. For approved veterans, VA pays for a commercial veterinary insurance policy for one service dog at a time, covering medically necessary treatment and prescription medications. Costs above the policy's limits are the veteran's responsibility. VA also covers hardware the dog needs to perform its tasks, such as a harness, through Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service, plus travel costs to obtain a dog or a replacement dog.
  • Not covered, per VA: license tags, nonprescription food, grooming, boarding, over-the-counter medications and similar items.
  • Who qualifies: veterans prescribed a guide or service dog by a VA clinical team for a visual, hearing or substantial mobility impairment. VA also lists a mental health mobility pathway: a VA mental health provider decides whether a mental health condition is the main cause of a substantial mobility limitation and whether a mobility service dog is the best treatment. The dog and veteran must have completed training with an Assistance Dogs International (ADI) or International Guide Dog Federation (IGDF) accredited organization, with a certificate.
  • How to start: talk to your VA clinical care provider, or a VA mental health provider for the mental health mobility pathway.
  • Source: VA Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service — Service Dog Veterinary Health Benefit (page last updated May 27, 2025) · Checked Nov 9, 2026.

2. PTSD alone

  • VA's National Center for PTSD states that VA does not provide service dogs for any condition, including PTSD, and that there is not yet enough research to know whether dogs help treat PTSD. It says VA would provide veterinary care for PTSD service dogs if research supports their use.
  • Source: VA National Center for PTSD — Dogs and PTSD · Checked Nov 9, 2026.

3. PAWS for Veterans Therapy Act — PAWS pilot program

  • What it is (law): the PAWS Act requires VA to run a five-year pilot program offering dog training to eligible veterans with PTSD, as part of complementary and integrative health care. VA lists five pilot sites: Asheville (NC), West Palm Beach (FL), San Antonio (TX), Palo Alto (CA) and Anchorage (AK).
  • What it is not: participants train service dogs for other veterans. VA states you will not receive a service dog from the pilot, and that it does not replace evidence-based PTSD treatment.
  • Who qualifies (VA Palo Alto's published criteria): an outpatient veteran with current PTSD symptoms, enrolled at a pilot-site VA medical center, seen by primary care, mental health or similar services within the last 3 months, screened and approved by the PAWS psychologist, and able to attend 8 weekly sessions. Other sites may word their criteria differently.
  • Sources: VA News — PAWS: Puppies Assisting Wounded Servicemembers (Sep 25, 2024) · VA Palo Alto — PAWS pilot program (updated Apr 29, 2026) · Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Canada — Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC)

4. VAC treatment benefits and psychiatric service dogs

  • Current position (official): VAC's treatment benefits do not recognize psychiatric service dogs as a mental health treatment, and VAC does not fund expenses for emotional support animals. VAC says it continues to review the evidence, including the US VA pilot.
  • Background: VAC funded a 2015 pilot study on service dogs for veterans with PTSD (31 recruited, 18 completed). VAC calls its findings promising but limited by the small sample.
  • Sources: VAC — Service dog pilot study FAQ (date modified 2024-05-28) · VAC — Standing Committee briefing binder, Feb 5, 2026, "Service Dogs" note (PDF) · Checked Nov 9, 2026.

5. Medical Expense Tax Credit (Canada Revenue Agency): a tax credit, not a VAC benefit

  • What it is: the cost of a specially trained service animal can be an eligible medical expense for people in the situations CRA lists. These include severe mental impairment, for expenses after 2017; blindness; profound deafness; and severe, prolonged impairments that markedly restrict the use of arms or legs. The animal must be specially trained to perform specific tasks. An animal that only provides emotional support does not qualify.

  • Who qualifies: set by CRA rules. VAC notes the credit is available to eligible Canadians, including veterans.

  • Source: CRA — Details of medical expenses (Service animals) · Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Finding an accredited training organization (optional line)

VA's veterinary benefit requires training through an ADI- or IGDF-accredited organization. Assistance Dogs International publishes a member search with filters, including one for programs serving veterans. PetPolicyCheck does not recommend or rank any trainer or program. Contact organizations directly about eligibility, wait times and costs. Checked Nov 9, 2026.

Information only, not legal, medical or tax advice. Rules and programs change: confirm with your airline, VA, VAC or CRA before you rely on them.

Veteran snowbirds & VA / military lodging

  • Canada → US (snowbird): CDC Dog Import Form receipt — often usable for months for dogs from dog-rabies-free/low-risk countries (confirm CDC). Keep rabies docs current.
  • Mexico: SENASICA/OISA entry. Dogs entering or returning to the US from Mexico require screwworm-freedom certification based on inspection within five days before shipment. Mexico also requires a separate NWS inspection document for pet dogs originating in and travelling from the US, effective August 3, 2026. Check the required signatory and document for each direction.
  • Fisher House: Fisher House is not ordinary pet-friendly lodging. For example, VA South Texas does not allow pets and requires prior approval for the registered guest’s task-trained service animal. Confirm eligibility and the specific house’s service-animal procedure before arrival.
  • Navy Lodge / IHG Army Hotels: service dogs free. Companion-pet availability, room allocation, fees and size restrictions depend on the property. Navy Lodge asks guests to register pets and contact the lodge for multiple pets or pets over 70 lb. IHG Army Hotels asks guests to notify reservations and check its pet policy. A room booking does not grant base access.
