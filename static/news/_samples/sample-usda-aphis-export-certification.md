On its official page [Take a Pet From the United States to Another Country (Export)](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/us-to-another-country-export) (last modified 14 August 2026), USDA APHIS states that the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) is the primary Government authority for all dogs imported into the United States, including returning U.S.-origin dogs, and that the following rabies-related requirements are established by CDC, not APHIS:

- Dogs traveling to or returning from a high-risk country for dog rabies must have the “Certification of U.S.-Issued Rabies Vaccination” form completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and submitted before departing the United States.
- This form cannot be issued retroactively after your dog leaves the United States.

The same APHIS page also advises pet owners planning to take a pet from the United States to another country to contact a USDA-accredited veterinarian as soon as they decide to travel, so the veterinarian can help determine the destination country’s entry requirements and obtain a USDA-endorsed pet health certificate or other needed paperwork. Destination country requirements can change at any time and must be verified for each trip.

Facts on this sample were checked against the official APHIS page on 20 November 2026. Rules can change; confirm on the official source before you travel.
