Flying with a dog or cat from the USA to Japan

What your dog or cat needs to fly from the United States to Japan without a long quarantine, and what you need to bring it back. Japan’s rules come from the Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF); USDA APHIS endorses the US health certificate. By our count you need at least about 7 months from the first rabies shot to arrival (30 days between shots, then the 180-day wait), so start early.

Quick answer

  • Dogs and cats from the US mainland: Japan treats the US (except Hawaii and Guam) as a non-designated region: ISO microchip, two rabies shots, a rabies titer (0.5 IU/ml or more) at an approved lab, then a 180-day wait before arrival. If everything is in order, quarantine is within 12 hours. Source
  • Time windows: Notify Japan's Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) at least 40 days before arrival; vet exam within 10 days before boarding; the titer result is valid 2 years from the blood draw. Source
  • Paperwork: A USDA-accredited vet completes the health certificate (Form AC) and submits it through VEHCS for USDA APHIS endorsement. APHIS endorses dog and cat certificates to Japan digitally; carry a printed copy. Source
  • From Hawaii or Guam: Hawaii and Guam are rabies-free designated regions: microchip, 180 days' residence (or since birth), 40-day notice, vet exam within 10 days and Form AB. No titer test or 180-day wait. Source
  • If something is missing: The pet is held at an AQS facility for up to 180 days, at your cost, or sent back. Source
  • Coming back to the US: Japan is not on the CDC high-risk list, so a dog needs only the free CDC Dog Import Form receipt, a microchip, a healthy look and an age of 6 months or more (if it was only in low-risk countries in the past 6 months). Source

Rules change. Always confirm with Japan’s AQS and USDA APHIS before you travel; a missing date or signature can mean up to 180 days of quarantine. Last checked Oct 24, 2026.

Will your US pet insurance work in Japan?

Often not. Of the 11 US insurers we checked, most limit coverage to the US and Canada (some add US territories or military bases); only Embrace and Nationwide say they cover vet care abroad, and terms apply. Policies reimburse you after you pay the vet, so keep itemized invoices and records.

Coming back to the USA

Japan is not on the CDC list of high-risk countries for dog rabies, so a dog that was only in Japan (or other low-risk countries) in the 6 months before returning needs the free CDC Dog Import Form receipt, a microchip, a healthy look and an age of at least 6 months; the CDC simplified this on August 1, 2024. Cats have no federal rabies proof requirement. State and airline rules still apply. CDC · CDC high-risk list
