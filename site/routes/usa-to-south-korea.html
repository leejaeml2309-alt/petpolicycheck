Traveling to South Korea with your dog or cat

What your dog or cat needs to fly from the United States to South Korea, how to get around Korea with it, and what you need to bring it back. Korea’s import rules come from the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA); USDA APHIS endorses the US health certificate. Book the rabies antibody (FAVN) test early: the result has to be on the health certificate.

Quick answer

  • Dogs and cats 90 days or older from the US: ISO 11784/11785 microchip, current rabies vaccination given before arrival, and a rabies-neutralizing antibody (FAVN) result of 0.5 IU/ml or more from an internationally approved lab, with the blood drawn within 24 months. If everything checks out, the pet can be released on the day of arrival. Source
  • Paperwork: A health certificate issued by a USDA-accredited vet and endorsed by USDA APHIS within 30 days of export. The vet can submit it through VEHCS, but APHIS must ink-sign and emboss it, and the original must travel with your pet. Source
  • Puppies and kittens under 90 days: No rabies vaccination or antibody test needed, but they still need an ISO microchip and a certificate showing the chip number and birth date. Source
  • Advance notice: Only needed when you bring 10 or more dogs and/or cats: send APQA an advance notification and get approval for a quarantine facility first. Source
  • If something is missing: No microchip, a chip number that doesn't match, no antibody test or a result under 0.5 IU/ml: the pet is held in quarantine at your expense (APQA says 10 days on average, sometimes longer). With no health certificate, the pet is sent back at your cost. Source
  • Coming back to the US: South Korea is not on the CDC high-risk list for dog rabies, so a dog that was only in low-risk countries in the past 6 months needs the free CDC Dog Import Form receipt, a microchip, a healthy look and an age of 6 months or more. Source

Rules change. Always confirm with APQA and USDA APHIS before you travel; a missing microchip, test result or endorsement can mean quarantine at your cost, or your pet being sent back. Last checked Oct 24, 2026.

Will your US pet insurance work in South Korea?

Often not. Of the 11 US insurers we checked, most limit coverage to the US and Canada (some add US territories or military bases); only Embrace and Nationwide say they cover vet care abroad, and terms apply. Policies reimburse you after you pay the vet, so keep itemized invoices and records.

Getting around Korea with a pet

Last checked Oct 24, 2026. Korean-language official sources, summarized in English.

ModeRuleSource
Korail trains (KTX and others)Korail's FAQ: keep your pet in a dedicated carrier so it doesn't bother other passengers; pets may travel if they have the necessary vaccinations, such as rabies. If your pet needs its own seat, you buy an adult fare for it.Korail
Trains: Korean lawKorea's official legal information site adds that the pet and its carrier must not take up a seat or the aisle and must stay inside the carrier, out of sight. A passenger who breaks the rules and harms others can be removed from the train or fined.Easy Law (MOLEG)
TaxisWhether a taxi takes your pet is up to each taxi company's terms of carriage, so ask before you ride. Registered assistance dogs with their handler can't be refused without a lawful reason.Easy Law (MOLEG)
Subway, busesSubways: the pet must be in a carrier, out of sight and without unpleasant odor. City and express buses: rules vary by bus company, and most accept small pets in a carrier within weight and size limits; check with the company first.Easy Law (MOLEG)

Coming back to the USA

South Korea is not on the CDC list of high-risk countries for dog rabies (North Korea is), so a dog that was only in South Korea (or other low-risk countries) in the 6 months before returning needs the free CDC Dog Import Form receipt, a microchip, a healthy look and an age of at least 6 months. Cats have no federal rabies proof requirement. State and airline rules still apply. CDC · CDC high-risk list
