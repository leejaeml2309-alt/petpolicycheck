Pet travel rules for Italy: EU entry and travellers’ points of entry

Italy applies the EU pet-entry rules for dogs, cats and ferrets, with a few national points of its own: Italy does not allow the puppy rabies derogation, and it does not require tick or tapeworm treatment for entry. The EU framework in force since 22 April 2026 is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/705 (certificate models) and 2026/636 (listed countries). Checked 11 November 2026. Always confirm with the Italian Ministry of Health, the travellers’ point of entry and your airline before you travel.

Every entry rule below is labelled Official rule. Nothing on this page is labelled Field-Verified. The Ministry of Health page “In viaggio verso l’Italia” was last updated on 3 July 2024 and still cites Regulations (EU) 576/2013 and 577/2013, which ceased to apply on 21 April 2026. USDA APHIS, on its Italy page last modified 2 September 2026, also still refers to “EU 576/2013 Annex III” for rabies requirements, while describing the new EU certificates under EU 2026/131. Where that matters, it is flagged. Follow the current Commission and APHIS certificate models.

Entry rules (Ministry of Health)

Authority: Italian Ministry of Health — In viaggio verso l’Italia (updated 3 July 2024). EU rules: European Commission — Bringing a pet into the EU from a non-EU country and the Commission list of territories and non-EU countries under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (Commission pages last confirmed 10 November 2026). For pets leaving the United States: USDA APHIS — Pet travel from the United States to Italy (dateModified 2 September 2026; checked 11 November 2026).

Dogs

  • Identification. Official rule An ISO transponder (microchip) meeting ISO 11784 and ISO 11785, implanted before or at the same time as the rabies vaccination. A tattoo is accepted only if applied before 3 July 2011 and still clearly readable. Ministry of Health · APHIS Italy · Commission
  • Rabies vaccination. Official rule An inactivated or recombinant vaccine given at 12 weeks of age or older, after identification, by an authorised veterinarian. After a primary vaccination, wait at least 21 days — or the manufacturer’s recommended immunity period if it is longer — before entry. APHIS treats a primary rabies vaccination given in the United States as valid for 1 year for EU purposes, even if a 3-year product was used; keep boosters without a gap. The Ministry of Health page still cites Annex III to Regulation (EU) 576/2013 for this waiting period; APHIS does the same. The Commission guide in force since 22 April 2026 states the same 12-week minimum and 21-day wait. Ministry of Health · APHIS Italy · Commission
  • Rabies titer. Official rule Required only when the pet comes from a territory or non-EU country that is not listed in Annex I or Annex II of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. The blood sample is taken at least 30 days after the primary vaccination, the result must be at least 0.5 IU/ml from an EU-designated laboratory, and the animal may enter only after 3 months from the sample date. The United States, Canada, Mexico, Great Britain and Japan are listed (no titer). South Korea does not appear on that Commission list. Commission listing (confirmed 10 November 2026)
  • EU animal health certificate. Official rule From a non-EU country, other than the Annex I territories treated as equivalent to intra-EU movement, the pet travels with an EU animal health certificate — current model in Annex III to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 — issued by an official veterinarian or issued by an authorised veterinarian and endorsed by the competent authority, plus a written non-commercial declaration. The Commission states that the certificate is valid for 10 days from issue until the checks at the travellers’ point of entry (extended by the length of a sea journey). After those checks, it can be used for later travel to other EU countries for 6 months or until the rabies vaccination expires, whichever is earlier. EU residents use an EU pet passport. Commission
  • Number of pets and accompaniment. Official rule Non-commercial movement: a maximum of 5 dogs, cats or ferrets with the owner or an authorised person, unless the animals are over 6 months old and travelling for a documented competition, exhibition or sporting event. The owner or authorised person should normally travel with the pet; for justified reasons the pet may travel up to 5 days before or after, or arrive at a different place. Ministry of Health
  • Minimum age. Official rule Italy does not use the optional derogation that would let unvaccinated or not-yet-protected puppies enter. The ministry prohibits entry of dogs, cats and ferrets younger than 12 weeks that have not been vaccinated against rabies, and of animals aged between 12 and 16 weeks that have been vaccinated but have not yet completed the 21-day validity wait. The practical minimum age from a listed country is therefore about 15 weeks (12 weeks plus 21 days). From an unlisted country the wait after the titer sample adds 3 months. Ministry of Health
  • Tapeworm and ticks. Official rule Preventive treatment against ticks or Echinococcus is not required to enter Italy. A later dog tapeworm treatment is required only if you then travel on to Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland or Norway (24–120 hours before entry to those destinations). Ministry of Health · Commission

Cats

  • Official rule The same identification, rabies, listed/unlisted titer, certificate or passport, accompaniment and age rules apply to cats (and ferrets) as to dogs. Ministry of Health · Commission

These rules cover non-commercial movements with the owner or a designated person. Animals intended for sale, transfer of ownership or adoption follow the commercial procedures, which this page does not set out. Ministry of Health

Arriving from the USA, Canada, Japan, South Korea or the EU

From the United States

  • Official rule The United States is listed in Annex II of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 — no rabies titer. Commission listing
  • Official rule A USDA-accredited veterinarian issues the EU non-commercial certificate and submits it through VEHCS. APHIS must ink-sign and emboss the certificate (electronic signature is accepted from the accredited veterinarian; original ink endorsement is required from APHIS). The endorsed original must travel with the pet. APHIS requires the endorsement to fall within 10 days of the pet’s arrival in the EU. It also states that the non-commercial certificate is valid for 30 days after the accredited veterinarian issues it, and that it may then be used for travel within the EU for up to 6 months from the day of entry, while the rabies vaccination remains valid. The owner or designated person must complete and sign the declaration, which travels with the certificate. New non-commercial certificates took effect on 1 October 2026 (APHIS cites EU 2026/131). If the pet already holds a valid EU pet passport, an export health certificate may not be needed — confirm on the APHIS page. APHIS Italy (dateModified 2 September 2026; checked 11 November 2026)

From Canada

  • Official rule Canada is listed — no rabies titer. The pet still needs the ISO microchip, a valid rabies vaccination with the 21-day wait after a primary course, the EU animal health certificate and declaration, and entry through a travellers’ point of entry. Commission listing

From Japan

  • Official rule Japan is listed — no rabies titer for entry to the EU. The same certificate, microchip and rabies-vaccination rules apply. Commission listing

From South Korea

  • Official rule The Republic of Korea (South Korea) does not appear in Annex I or Annex II on the Commission listing page last confirmed on 10 November 2026. Pets coming from Korea need a rabies antibody titration (sample at least 30 days after vaccination, result at least 0.5 IU/ml at an EU-designated laboratory) and a 3-month wait from the sample date, plus the EU animal health certificate and declaration. Commission listing

From another EU country, or from an Annex I territory

  • Official rule From an EU member state, the pet needs identification, a valid rabies vaccination and an EU pet passport. The ministry also applies the same intra-EU conditions, where the Commission has found the rules equivalent, to movements from the Annex I territories listed with Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (Andorra, Switzerland, the Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Greenland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City State; Norway and Northern Ireland are treated as equivalent for these health purposes). Ministry of Health · Commission listing

Where you enter

  • Official rule From a non-EU country other than the Annex I territories, the dog, cat or ferret must enter through a travellers’ point of entry designated by Italy, and be presented for documentary and identity checks. Commission — Travellers’ points of entry
  • Official rule The latest official Italian list cited on this page is dated January 2021 and is still titled under Article 34 of Regulation (EU) 576/2013. It names 41 airports and a series of ports (numbered 42 to 65 on that PDF). Confirm the current list before you travel, because the document is older than the 22 April 2026 EU rules. Ministry of Health — points of entry (PDF, January 2021)

Confirm the port’s own definition of transit before you rely on that exemption. APHIS Italy

National rules in Italy

  • Official rule The Minister of Health ordinance of 6 August 2013 on protecting public safety from dog attacks, as later modified, remains in force. It was prorogued for a further 12 months from 5 September 2026 by the ordinance of 16 July 2026 (Gazzetta Ufficiale, General Series no. 206 of 5 September 2026), so the current term runs to 5 September 2027. The 2026 act is a prorogation only; the operative duties are in the 2013 ordinance as later modified. Ordinance 16 July 2026 (26A04624) · Ordinance 6 August 2013 (13A07313), GU no. 209 of 6 September 2013
  • Municipal and regional rules can add local leash, muzzle or access conditions. This page does not invent those local rules.
  • Official rule Italy does not require tick or Echinococcus treatment for entry, as already noted above. Ministry of Health

Airlines and ferry operators set their own pet-carriage rules, in addition to the official entry rules. APHIS Italy. For IATA-style hard crates used in the hold, see the existing IATA hard crates for cargo product page.

Airports of national interest (ENAC, 39)

Official rule ENAC’s page “Aeroporti in Italia”, updated 23 June 2026, lists 39 airports of national interest under d.P.R. no. 201/2015. Three are marked as strategic international hubs (*): Milano Malpensa, Roma Fiumicino and Venezia. Ten further airports are marked strategic (**): Bari, Bologna, Cagliari, Catania, Firenze, Lamezia Terme, Napoli, Palermo, Pisa and Torino. ENAC — Aeroporti in Italia

IATA and ICAO codes, and the official airport names in the table, are taken from ENAC’s Dati di traffico 2025, which also lists 44 airports open to commercial civil traffic. The five commercial airports that are not on the national-interest list of 39 are Albenga (ALL), Aosta (AOT), Bolzano (BZO), Forlì (FRL) and Marina di Campo / Elba (EBA). Return to the United States

Official rule CDC: every dog entering the United States needs a CDC Dog Import Form receipt, must be 6 months or older, must have a microchip, and must appear healthy. Italy is not on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list dated 15 April 2026 (checked 11 November 2026), provided the dog has not been in a high-risk country in the past 6 months. Cats: appear healthy at federal level; state and airline rules still apply. An EU pet passport does not replace the CDC Dog Import Form for dogs. FAQ

Do I need a rabies titer for Italy from the United States, Canada or Japan?

Official rule No. Those countries are listed under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. South Korea is not listed, so a titer and a 3-month wait apply from Korea.

Which certificate model should I use in 2026?

Official rule The current EU non-commercial model is Annex III of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 (in force since 22 April 2026). APHIS required the new non-commercial form from 1 October 2026. The Italian ministry page dated 3 July 2024 still cites Regulations (EU) 576/2013 and 577/2013. Follow the current Commission and APHIS model.

Can I fly into any of ENAC’s 39 airports with a pet from the United States?

Not automatically. From a third country the pet must enter through a designated travellers’ point of entry. The latest official Italian list cited on this page is dated January 2021 and is not the same as ENAC’s 39 airports of national interest. Check that list, and your airline, before you book.

Does Italy require tapeworm treatment?

Official rule Not for entry into Italy. A dog tapeworm treatment 24–120 hours beforehand is required only if you then enter Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland or Norway.
