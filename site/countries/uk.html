Pet travel rules for the United Kingdom: entering Great Britain with a dog or cat

Rules from GOV.UK (Defra / APHA) for bringing a pet dog, cat or ferret into Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales). Northern Ireland follows EU pet rules (DAERA), not these GB rules. Checked 10 November 2026. Always confirm with GOV.UK and your airline before you fly.

Every entry rule below is labelled Official rule. Nothing on this page is labelled Field-Verified. Where a fee or a detail is not on the official page we checked, we say so instead of guessing.

Entry rules (Defra / APHA)

Authority: GOV.UK — Bringing your pet dog, cat or ferret to Great Britain. These steps apply if you are bringing pet dogs, cats or ferrets into England, Wales or Scotland, including with a guide or assistance dog.

Dogs

Microchip
Official rule The pet must be microchipped before the rabies vaccination. If the chip is implanted after the vaccine, the pet must be vaccinated again. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
Rabies
Official rule Vaccinate against rabies. How long you must wait before travelling depends on the vaccine used. This will be at least 21 full days after the first vaccination (or the last of the first course). Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
Rabies blood test (titer)
Official rule Needed only if you are travelling from a country that is not part of the EU and is not listed. Sample at least 30 days after vaccination, tested at an EU-approved lab, result at least 0.5 IU/ml, then wait 3 months from the sample date. The USA, Canada and Mexico are listed (no titer). South Korea does not appear on the GOV.UK listed-countries table. Blood tests · Listed countries Checked 10 Nov 2026
Health certificate
Official rule From the rest of the world you need a Great Britain pet health certificate signed, stamped and dated by an official veterinarian. The pet must enter Great Britain within 10 days of the certificate being issued. From an EU country you may use an EU pet passport (or certain other documents listed on GOV.UK). From Northern Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man, no passport or health certificate is needed to enter Great Britain. GB certificate · Which document Checked 10 Nov 2026
Tapeworm (dogs only)
Official rule A vet must treat the dog for tapeworm every time you bring it to Great Britain, with praziquantel or an equivalent proven against Echinococcus multilocularis, and record product, manufacturer, date and time, stamp and signature. Treatment must be no less than 24 hours and no more than 5 days (120 hours) before you arrive. Exempt if you are coming directly from Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland, Malta or Norway. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
Declaration / commercial
Official rule Fill in a declaration if you are not going to sell or transfer ownership. Extra “Balai” (commercial) rules apply if you will sell, rehome or transfer the pet, if the pet arrives more than 5 days before or after you, or if you bring more than 5 pets and are not attending or training for a competition, show or sporting event. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
  • Official rule If you do not follow the rules, the pet may be put into quarantine for up to 4 months — or refused entry if you travelled by sea — at your cost. Source
  • Official rule Someone else may travel with your pet if you have authorised it in writing. Source

Cats

Microchip, rabies, documents
Official rule Same microchip-before-rabies rule, same minimum 21-day wait, and the same travel document rules as for dogs (listed vs not listed; GB pet health certificate within 10 days from the rest of the world). Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
Tapeworm
Official rule The tapeworm treatment page applies to dogs. GOV.UK does not require this treatment for cats. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026

Arriving from the USA, South Korea or the EU

From the United States

  • Official rule The USA (including American Samoa, Guam, Northern Mariana Islands, Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands) is a listed country. Use a Great Britain pet health certificate. No rabies blood test. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
  • Official rule USDA APHIS: a USDA-accredited veterinarian completes the correct GB certificate and submits it via VEHCS. APHIS requires original ink endorsement (not digital-only). Arrive in Great Britain within 10 days of APHIS endorsement. USDA APHIS dateModified 6 Oct 2026; checked 10 Nov 2026
  • See also the route page USA ↔ United Kingdom.

From South Korea

Official rule South Korea (Republic of Korea) does not appear in the GOV.UK table of listed countries fetched on 10 November 2026. Pets travelling from an unlisted, non-EU country need a Great Britain pet health certificate and a rabies blood test (sample at least 30 days after vaccination, result at least 0.5 IU/ml at an EU-approved lab, then a 3-month wait from the sample date). Listed countries · Blood tests

Korean Air is on the GOV.UK approved commercial-airline list to London Heathrow (HARC), updated 9 November 2026. You must still use an approved route and follow cargo rules.

Official rule Before you leave South Korea, apply to the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA/QIA) for export quarantine. For export, the official application page asks for a vaccination certificate and a veterinary document stating that the animal is healthy and there is no risk of spreading a livestock infectious disease, plus any extra papers the destination country requires. The stated fee for a dog or cat is 10,000 won per application (other species have different fees on the same page). You may apply in person, by post, by fax or online; the stated processing time is 1 day. QIA also publishes country-specific export guidance, including sheets for the UK (England, Wales and Scotland) and the EU — check the sheet for your destination, because foreign rules can change without notice. Some regional offices require a reservation at least one day ahead (same-day reservation not available). Use the export-pet quarantine reservation system (QIA announced a move to eminwon.apqa.go.kr/reservation from 23 October 2026). QIA: country-specific pet export guidance · QIA e-minwon: animal quarantine application Checked 10 Nov 2026

From the EU

  • Official rule From an EU country the pet must have one of: a pet passport issued in an EU country or certain other countries; an animal health certificate issued in Great Britain in the last 6 months; a Great Britain pet health certificate; or a pet passport issued in Great Britain before 1 January 2021. Source Checked 10 Nov 2026
  • Official rule Dogs still need tapeworm treatment unless arriving directly from Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland, Malta or Norway. Source

Approved routes — you cannot just land anywhere

  • Official rule You can only use certain travel routes and companies to enter Great Britain. Check the approved list before you book — it can change or operate only at certain times of year. You do not need an approved route if you are travelling within the UK or from Ireland. Travel routes Checked 10 Nov 2026
  • Official rule If you are travelling by plane, pets have to travel as cargo unless you are flying on a chartered private plane or travelling with a guide or assistance dog. Source
  • Official rule You can only bring a pet into Great Britain by private boat if you are travelling from Ireland or Northern Ireland. Source
  • Official rule Approved commercial air routes (list updated 9 November 2026) send pets in the hold to combinations that include London Heathrow (HARC or AAC), London Gatwick (AAC), Manchester (Pets on Jets), Edinburgh, Glasgow and Newcastle (ARC). Birmingham appears for charter operators, not as a scheduled commercial airline destination on that list. Airlines and airports you can use
  • Official rule Sea and rail: approved ferry companies and Eurotunnel LeShuttle are listed on GOV.UK. The LeShuttle operator page fetched 10 November 2026 states that taking a pet costs £24 each way, and that registered guide and assistance dogs travel free (present an official registration certificate at pet reception on both the UK and French terminals, together with the dog’s travel documents). A maximum of five pets per vehicle can travel; dogs, cats and ferrets must be more than 12 weeks old. After check-in, go to Pet Reception; check the pet in at least 1 hour and no more than 2 hours before the booked departure, and bring the booking reference. The pet stays in the car with you for the crossing. LeShuttle: taking your pet abroad · GOV.UK sea routes Checked 10 Nov 2026

Banned types of dog

Official rule You cannot bring a banned type of dog into Great Britain unless it already has a valid Certificate of Exemption. Banned types (judged by appearance, not by papers or name) are: Pit Bull Terrier, Japanese Tosa, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro and XL Bully. It is also against the law to sell, abandon, give away or breed from a banned dog. GOV.UK: Banned dogs Checked 10 Nov 2026

Official rule England and Wales — XL Bully: it is a criminal offence to own or possess an XL Bully without a valid Certificate of Exemption. Applications have closed; new exemptions can only be authorised by a court order. Ban on XL Bully dogs Last updated 1 October 2026; checked 10 Nov 2026

Official rule From 1 November 2026 you must not leave a child under 12 in close contact with a banned dog in a private place (for example a home or garden) without adult supervision. Source

Scotland has its own XL Bully rules: gov.scot dateModified 25 September 2026. Northern Ireland also has separate guidance — do not mix NI and GB regimes.

Return to the United States

Official rule CDC (since 1 August 2024): every dog entering or returning to the US needs a CDC Dog Import Form receipt, must be 6 months or older, microchipped, and appear healthy. The UK is not on the CDC high-risk dog-rabies list, so no extra CDC rabies document is needed if the dog spent the past 6 months only in rabies-free or low-risk countries. Cats: appear healthy at federal level; check airline and US state rules. CDC · APHIS cats Checked 10 Nov 2026

Official rule GOV.UK “taking your pet abroad” focuses on travel to the EU with an animal health certificate. A mandatory GB exit health certificate solely for return to the US is not stated on the official GB pages reviewed — confirm with the APHA Pet Travel helpline and your airline before departure. Source

Taxi and private hire (VTC)

London black cabs (TfL)

Official rule The TfL London taxi (black cab) driver handbook (version dated 29 July 2025; PDF fetched 10 November 2026) says licensed taxi drivers must carry a disabled person’s assistance dog and allow it to remain with the passenger at all times, and must not charge any more for carrying an assistance dog. A medical exemption (for example severe asthma, allergy or an acute phobia of dogs) is possible only if TfL has issued a certificate the driver must carry and a sign that must be displayed when plying for hire. Drivers are not compelled to accept a hiring for luggage and/or animals unaccompanied by a passenger, or to carry items or animals likely to cause damage to the taxi or its fittings — “However, you must take guide dogs and other assistance dogs.” The handbook does not set a general duty to accept every pet; for an ordinary dog or cat, ask the driver. TfL taxi driver handbook (PDF) · TfL assistance dogs Checked 10 Nov 2026

Uber Pet (United Kingdom)

Official rule Uber publishes a UK rider page for Uber Pet. Where the option appears in the app, you can request a pet-friendly ride. Uber says you can travel with up to 3 passengers and 1 pet, and that you can bring one pet per trip, such as a dog or a cat; availability may vary by location. Use a lead, harness or carrier for a dog or cat; other pets should travel in a carrier. Stay with your pet. Uber says journeys “may include an additional fee compared to other ride options” and a cleaning fee may apply if the pet leaves waste, excessive hair or causes damage. The rider page does not publish a fixed extra amount — check the upfront price in the app before you request. (Driver-facing extras are not used as the rider surcharge.) You do not need to select Uber Pet when travelling with a service animal; you can request any ride option. Uber Pet (UK) Checked 10 Nov 2026

Bus (TfL and National Express)

TfL buses, Tube, tram, DLR, London Overground and Elizabeth line

Official rule TfL Conditions of Carriage (24 September 2026 until further notice), §§9.15–9.17: you can take an assistance dog on bus, Tube, tram, DLR, London Overground and Elizabeth line services. You can also take any other dog or inoffensive animal unless there is a good reason to refuse it (for example if the animal seems dangerous or not properly controlled). You must keep it under control on a lead or in a suitable container and must not allow it on a seat. Staff are not allowed to take charge of any animal. At stations use the wide gates or carry the animal through the gates; if you have an assistance dog and there is no wide gate, ask staff to open the gates. Use a staircase or lift where provided; if you must use a moving escalator, carry the animal unless you have an assistance dog trained to walk on moving escalators; if the animal is too large to carry, staff will stop the escalator when it is safe (generally outside rush hours and when the station is not busy). TfL Conditions of Carriage (PDF fetched 10 November 2026)

A public “Pets on public transport” page also exists (tfl.gov.uk, HTTP 200). The dated Conditions of Carriage (24 September 2026) are the official source used on this page; that extract does not state a separate pet fare. Checked 10 Nov 2026

National Express coaches

Official rule National Express Conditions of Carriage welcome highly trained assistance dogs and assistance animals on coaches and recommend that customers pre-book. You will be asked to provide information that the animal is highly trained (for example a certificate, correspondence from the training organisation, an ID book, owner training logs, an email or a verbal assessment). The operator requests that assistance dogs or animals wear a safety harness or travel in a pet carrier that can fit on a seat and be attached to a spare seat belt (you provide the harness or carrier). Where applicable, an assistance dog must be transported in line with the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991. The luggage policy lists “Any pets, other than assistance dogs” among items that are not carried. The accessibility page also lists restricted animals, including puppies under 12 weeks and other juvenile animals, birds or flying mammals, non-domesticated/“wild” animals, reptiles, insects, spiders, amphibians, fish, and livestock (the list is not exhaustive). Conditions of carriage · Luggage · Accessibility Checked 10 Nov 2026

Train (National Rail, LNER, Eurostar)

National Rail

Official rule National Rail Conditions of Travel (from 30 July 2026), Condition 24 (PDF fetched 10 November 2026): you may take up to two dogs or other small domestic animals free of charge unless a train company has set special conditions (those conditions will be available when you buy your ticket and on the company’s website). Animals, except assistance dogs, may not be taken into buffet or restaurant cars (including first-class accommodation with an at-seat meal service) unless the train company specifically allows it. Animals are not allowed on seats in any circumstances. Animals other than dogs must travel in a fully enclosed basket or pet carrier designed for the purpose, no larger than 85 × 60 × 60 cm and large enough for the animal to stand and lie down in comfort; animals too large for that size of carrier may not be conveyed by train. Dogs must be kept on a lead throughout the journey, including on station property; other animals must stay in their baskets or carriers. Staff may ask you to remove an animal that causes a nuisance. Special conditions apply to sleeper cabins — contact the train company before you book. Livestock and non-domestic animals cannot be carried. National Rail Conditions of Travel (July 2026)

The National Rail “Train Travel with Pets” page (fetched 10 November 2026) matches the two-pets-free rule and says that if you want to travel with more animals there may be an additional fee — contact the train company (no amount is stated on that page). It also says you are welcome to bring a pet on sleeper trains, that animals are not allowed in restaurant cars except assistance dogs, and that a sleeper journey with a dog may carry an additional charge for a heavy-duty clean (except assistance animals) — check with the train company; the amount is not published on that page — check with the train company. Assistance dogs always ride free and no cleaning charge is applied. National Rail: Train travel with pets

LNER

LNER publishes dedicated pet guidance at lner.co.uk/our-trains/travelling-with-pets. Apply the National Rail Conditions of Travel above and check LNER at booking. Policy details coming soon.

Eurostar

Official rule Pets are not allowed on trains to and from London, on trains between Brussels or the Netherlands and Lille, or on Eurostar Sun and Snow routes. Pets are welcome on other Eurostar routes between Belgium, France, Germany and the Netherlands. On those permitted routes, small domestic animals weighing less than 6 kg travel free in a carrier no larger than 45 × 30 × 25 cm. Larger dogs that do not fit in a carrier need a “dog” fare at a single price of €30, bought from the Train Manager; the dog must sit on your lap or on the floor and be leashed and muzzled. Guide dogs and assistance dogs and cats always travel free. Dangerous dogs, wild animals and birds are not permitted. Check that nearby passengers are comfortable with the pet; the Train Manager can find another seat if someone is nervous. Eurostar: travelling with pets Checked 10 Nov 2026

Official rule For a guide or assistance dog or assistance cat on a train to or from London, Eurostar asks customers to contact the company at least 24 hours before departure and book a space. There is no charge for the animal’s ticket. A maximum of four guide or assistance dogs or assistance cats are permitted per train. Eurostar books a spare seat next to the customer. You must arrive at the station at least 60 minutes before departure and see a member of Eurostar staff for final document checks before the ticket gates. Documents for UK↔EU travel match the GOV.UK list (microchip or a tattoo applied on or before 3 July 2011, valid rabies vaccination, dog tapeworm treatment when travelling to the UK, an animal health certificate unless you have a pet passport issued in an EU country or Northern Ireland, plus proof that Eurostar has accepted the animal). Dogs on the UK banned-type list and dogs in French categories 1 and 2 are not allowed on any Eurostar service, even if registered as a guide or assistance dog. On continental routes between France, Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, Eurostar says you do not need to provide documentation for a guide or assistance animal, they travel free without a ticket, and you should tell the train manager on board. Eurostar: assistance dogs and cats Checked 10 Nov 2026

Flights — UK airlines and Great Britain entry

Official rule Country rule first: on a commercial flight into Great Britain, pets have to travel as cargo unless you are flying on a chartered private plane or travelling with a guide or assistance dog. You must also use an approved route and company. That GOV.UK rule overrides any cabin offer an airline makes on other routes. GOV.UK travel routes

British Airways

Official rule BA does not carry pets in the cabin on any route. Pets travel in the hold, arranged by partners: PetAir UK (from the UK) or IAG Cargo (to the UK). Entering the UK, the dog must meet the DEFRA Pet Travel Scheme. BA cannot accept emotional support dogs in the cabin on BA-operated flights (they can travel as a pet). An assistance dog can travel with you free of charge in the cabin on the majority of BA flights. No cabin weight, carrier size or pet fee is published on that page (Policy details coming soon.). British Airways: travelling with pets Checked 10 Nov 2026 · See also the existing airline page British Airways pet policy (last verified 22 October 2026).

Virgin Atlantic

Official rule Virgin Atlantic Conditions of Carriage, Article 16 (fetched 10 November 2026): you must get the airline’s agreement before it will carry your pet or your assistance dog. Only assistance dogs accompanying disabled passengers can be taken into the aircraft cabin, and you must have the correct health and vaccination documents. The article points to Virgin’s assistance-dog help page for detailed procedures. Policy details coming soon. Virgin Atlantic: Article 16

easyJet

Official rule “Animals are not allowed on our flights, apart from recognised registered guide and assistance dogs.” See also easyJet’s special-assistance / service-dog policy. The restricted-items page fetched on 10 November 2026 does not list cabin weights or pet fees (there is no ordinary-pet product). Official rule On easyJet’s special-assistance page (fetched 10 November 2026), assistance dogs trained by an organisation accredited to Assistance Dogs International (ADI) or the International Guide Dog Federation (IGDF) can be accepted on permitted routes. Emotional support dogs and pets of any kind are forbidden. Assistance dogs are not permitted on flights to or from Egypt, Israel, Kosovo, Montenegro, Morocco or Turkey, because of local customs regulations. Provide health and training documents as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours before departure; the dog must wear a standard identifying jacket or harness and is carried free of charge together with containers and food, in addition to the baggage allowance. Arrive at bag drop or the special-assistance area at least 2 hours before departure. easyJet: restricted and unusual items · easyJet: special assistance Checked 10 Nov 2026

Jet2

Official rule “Where allowed, registered assistance dogs can be carried on our flights. We don’t carry any other animals onboard.” Registered assistance dogs must be trained by an organisation that is an accredited member of the International Guide Dog Federation (IGDF) or that meets the full membership criteria of Assistance Dogs International (ADI), and must be microchipped, vaccinated against rabies and hold a valid pet passport. Tell Jet2 in advance (at booking or through Manage My Booking). Assisted Travel requests should be made as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours before departure. No ordinary-pet cabin or hold product is offered, so there is no pet fare to publish. Jet2: Assisted travel Checked 10 Nov 2026

Hotels

Chain rules below are from official hotel pages fetched on 10 November 2026. They are not Field-Verified stays. Always check the individual hotel before you book.

  • Official rule Premier Inn: “Unfortunately, with the exception of trained assistance dogs, we don't allow pets to stay at our hotels.” Premier Inn: smoking & pet policies
  • Official rule Travelodge: the group offers pet-friendly hotels across the UK. Add your pet as an extra when you book on the Travelodge website. “A cleaning charge, payable upon booking, will be incurred per pet to account for a deep clean of the hotel room following check out. Guide dogs, assistance dogs, and hearing dogs are exempt from this charge.” The official page does not publish the amount of that cleaning charge — check it when you add the pet on the Travelodge booking path. Travelodge: pet-friendly hotels Checked 10 Nov 2026
  • Official rule Accor’s ibis / ibis Styles / ibis budget pet-friendly page (fetched 10 November 2026) welcomes dogs and cats in the room and in common areas (on a leash and supervised in common areas; not on furniture; not in kitchen areas; do not bathe the pet in the room shower or hotel pool). It states that a “small fee [is] charged per night”, and that amenities, variety and the fee are subject to each hotel’s policy. Look for the pet-friendly logo on the hotel’s services list. The page does not publish one group-wide euro amount. ibis pet-friendly hotels Checked 10 Nov 2026

Parks and dog spaces

Official rule The Royal Parks official leaflet “Dogs in The Royal Parks” (PDF in the December 2025 path, fetched 10 November 2026) says dogs are welcome in all the Royal Parks, but some places ban dogs or require a lead — usually ecologically sensitive sites, deer parks, children’s play areas, restaurants, cafés and some sports areas. In all Royal Parks, dogs are not permitted in children’s playgrounds or toilets, and they must be kept on a lead near roadways and near water. In Bushy Park and Richmond Park, dogs must be kept on a lead and away from the deer throughout the deer birthing season (1 May–31 July). You must keep your dog under control and clean up after it (the leaflet says you are breaking the law if you do not). The leaflet also lists park-by-park no-dog and on-lead areas (for example the Diana Memorial Fountain, Lido and Serpentine Lake in Hyde Park; Long Water, Round Pond and the Peter Pan statue in Kensington Gardens). The list is not exhaustive — follow on-site notices. Royal Parks: Dogs in The Royal Parks (PDF) Checked 10 Nov 2026

Grooming

UK groomers commonly ask for proof of core vaccinations; some add kennel cough (Bordetella). A foreign dog that only has a rabies record may still be refused. Call ahead and do not treat international-document acceptance as confirmed.

FAQ

Can my dog fly in the cabin to London?

Official rule Not on a commercial flight into Great Britain, unless you are travelling with a guide or assistance dog or on a chartered private plane. Pets travel as cargo on approved routes. Checked 10 Nov 2026

Does a US dog need a rabies titer for Great Britain?

Official rule No. The USA is a listed country. South Korea is not listed, so a titer and a 3-month wait apply from Korea. Checked 10 Nov 2026

Can I take Eurostar with my pet?

Official rule No, not as an ordinary pet on trains to or from London (or on Brussels/Netherlands–Lille, or Eurostar Sun and Snow). Assistance dogs and cats can travel on London trains if you contact Eurostar at least 24 hours ahead. Pets are welcome on other Eurostar routes between Belgium, France, Germany and the Netherlands (small animals under 6 kg free in a 45 × 30 × 25 cm carrier; larger dogs €30). That is a carrier rule, separate from GOV.UK entry rules. Eurostar Checked 10 Nov 2026

Is Northern Ireland the same as Great Britain?

Official rule No. Contact DAERA for travel to Northern Ireland. Do not mix the two regimes. DAERA
