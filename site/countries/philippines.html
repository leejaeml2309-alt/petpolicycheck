Pet travel rules for the Philippines: BAI import of dogs and cats

National rules from the Bureau of Animal Industry (BAI), Department of Agriculture. Checked 28 November 2026. Confirm with BAI and your airline before you travel.

Authority: BAI — current import rules and rules for SPSIC applications from 1 January 2027. From the United States, also see USDA APHIS: Pet Travel From the United States to the Philippines.

Entry rules

Every dog or cat that enters the Philippines needs an approved Sanitary and Phytosanitary Import Clearance (SPSIC) from BAI before travel. Apply online through the DA Trade System (InterCommerce): https://www.intercommerce.com.ph/registrationbai.asp.

Now

  1. Identify the dog or cat with a microchip. Collect vaccination and parasite records and a photograph.
  2. Give the required vaccinations, including rabies, at least 14 days before you apply. The animal must be at least 4 months old at application. A rabies antibody test (RNATT) is not required now. Dogs also need canine distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis, parainfluenza, and parvovirus vaccines; cats need feline panleukopenia.
  3. Apply for the SPSIC and allow 11 working days for processing. Maximum three animals per year, or the same three animals again if they are identified by microchip.
  4. Pregnant dogs or cats are not allowed. If the animal has given birth, the current BAI import page requires at least 12 weeks before travel.
  5. Travel only after the SPSIC is approved. Present the approved SPSIC and the official veterinary health certificate at the port of entry. For a United States origin shipment, a USDA-accredited veterinarian must issue the certificate and APHIS must endorse it.

From 1 January 2027

  1. Implant an ISO-compatible microchip before the blood draw for the RNATT.
  2. Give the rabies vaccination when the animal is at least 12 weeks old. Wait at least 30 days after a first vaccination, or after a late booster, before the blood draw. The RNATT result must be at least 0.5 IU/ml (FAVN or RFFIT only). A satisfactory result remains valid for life if every later rabies vaccination is given on time. Other vaccines are no longer required besides rabies.
  3. Complete a separate Form 1 and an individual photograph for each animal. Apply for the SPSIC when the animal is at least 18 weeks old. Allow up to 11 days from the “UNDER REVIEW” status. There is no limit on the number of dogs or cats if all requirements are met. An approved SPSIC is valid for 60 days, for one shipment only, and cannot be transferred or extended.
  4. Pregnant dogs or cats are not allowed. If the animal has given birth, the 2027 rules require 18 weeks before travel.
  5. Obtain the international veterinary health certificate (IVHC) within 10 days before export. BAI’s example: a certificate issued on 1 January 2027 is valid until 11 January 2027. The importer named on the SPSIC must travel with the animal. Travel before the “Must Ship Out Date.”
  6. On arrival, present the approved SPSIC, the original IVHC, Form 1, and the RNATT result. A government veterinary officer inspects the animals at the international port of entry. Fees in BAI’s 2027 guidance: SPSIC PHP 100.00, lodgement PHP 55.00, inspection PHP 250.00 per head for the first two pets and PHP 300.00 per head for each pet after that.

A SPSIC approved on or before 31 December 2026 may still be used within its 60-day validity, without the RNATT and Form 1.

Contact the National Veterinary Quarantine Services Division: nvqsd@bai.gov.ph.

Airlines · restrictions

Figures are from each airline’s official pet page, checked 28 November 2026. Confirm the operating carrier before you book.

United

Does not allow pets to, from, or through the Philippines.

Traveling with pets↗

EVA Air

Cabin: no, except service dogs.
Hold: yes — dogs, cats, and rabbits as checked baggage.
Ban: brachycephalic dogs and cats, and any mix, year-round to, from, or via Southeast Asia, including the Philippines.
Travelling with Pets↗

Cebu Pacific

Cabin: no for ordinary pets (service or emotional support dogs only, one per flight).
Hold: no — ordinary household pets go as cargo only.
Ban: snub-nosed pets are generally not accepted as cargo.
Household pets↗

Airlines · Asia

Philippine Airlines

Cabin: yes on domestic FurPAL only — dogs and cats, pet plus carrier 10 kg (22 lb) or less, one pet per passenger, PHP 2,500. Not available on PAL international flights.
Hold: yes — pet plus kennel 32 kg or less; over that, cargo.
Ban: snub-nosed breeds are not accepted as checked baggage.
Travelling with pets↗

Korean Air

Cabin: yes — pet plus carrier 7 kg (15 lb) or less; one animal per passenger; carrier up to 32 × 45 × 19 cm.
Hold: yes — pet plus carrier 45 kg (99 lb) or less; up to two in the hold per passenger.
Ban: named brachycephalic breeds, including pug, bulldog, and shih tzu.
Traveling with pets↗

Asiana

Cabin: yes — pet plus cage 7 kg or less; one in-cabin pet per adult.
Hold: yes — pet plus cage 45 kg or less; not on A321 international flights.
Ban: short-nosed animals have not been accepted as checked baggage since 1 July 2019.
Traveling with Pets↗

Japan Airlines

Cabin: no for ordinary pets on international flights.
Cargo: yes, with an advance reservation.
Ban: French Bulldogs and Bulldogs are not accepted.
Traveling with pets↗

ANA

Cabin: no for ordinary pets on ANA-operated flights.
Hold: yes — on ANA-operated flights, pet plus cage 45 kg (99 lb) or less; three sides 292 cm (115 in) or less.
Traveling with Pets↗

Cathay Pacific

Cargo: yes. From 1 September 2025, direct Cathay Cargo pet bookings are accepted only from approved agents or holders of valid IATA Live Animals Regulations training.
Travelling with animals↗

Singapore Airlines

Cabin: no — ordinary pets travel as checked baggage only.
Hold: yes — dogs and cats at least 3 months old; if pet plus container exceeds 32 kg, use a cargo agent. Not accepted on Boeing 737-operated flights.
Ban: named brachycephalic dog and cat breeds, including crossbreeds, are not accepted as checked baggage.
Travelling with pets↗

Airlines · US & Gulf

Delta

Cabin: yes — dogs and cats, $200 USD/CAD/EUR each way; kennel must fit under the seat (Delta recommends 18 × 11 × 11 in). Not permitted in Business Class, Delta One, or Delta Premium.
Cargo: only for certain U.S. military or State Department travelers, until further notice.
Ban: brachycephalic dogs, cats, and their mixes are not permitted on Delta or Delta Connection.
Pet Travel Overview↗

Emirates

Cabin: no, except falcons on certain Dubai–Pakistan flights and guide dogs.
Hold: yes if the itinerary starts in Dubai, the destination allows animals as excess baggage, and total journey time is under 17 hours (from USD 500 when animal plus cage do not exceed 23 kg and 150 cm).
Cargo: required if the itinerary ends in Dubai, exceeds 17 hours, or the destination does not allow animals as check-in baggage.
Rules and charges↗

Qatar Airways

Cabin: no for ordinary pets (falcons and service dogs only).
Hold: yes, up to 75 kg — USD 450 / CAD 590 on routes other than to or from Qatar (USD 300 / CAD 390 to or from Qatar). Over 75 kg or 300 cm, cargo.
