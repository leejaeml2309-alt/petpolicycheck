Pet travel rules for Mexico: SENASICA, OISA and the NWS screwworm certificate

Federal rules from SENASICA with OISA inspection at ports of entry. Federal rules re-verified 28 Nov 2026 (sources at the end of the page). Always confirm with SENASICA and your airline before you fly.

Federal rules (SENASICA / OISA)

Authority: SENASICA (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad, Inocuidad y Calidad Agroalimentaria) — OISA inspection offices at ports of entry.

Dogs (including NEW Aug 3, 2026 NWS rule)

Rabies
From the US or Canada: no rabies or health papers are asked for at entry (SENASICA rules as published by the Mexican consulate; APHIS lists none for US-origin dogs). From other countries: a current rabies vaccination (except pets under 3 months), recorded by the vet on a certificate of good health issued no more than 15 days before travel and a vaccination card.
Health / NWS certificate
From the US: no health certificate, but since 3 Aug 2026 an ‘NWS Inspection Certificate for Pet Dogs Moving (Exporting) from the United States to Mexico’, signed by a USDA-accredited vet no more than 5 days before entry. It needs no APHIS endorsement. From other countries: a certificate of good health issued no more than 15 days before travel, plus internal and external deworming within the previous 6 months.
Microchip
Not required by Mexico (required by CDC for US return).
Import form
Certificado Zoosanitario para Importación (CZI) issued at OISA on arrival after physical inspection.
  • Since 3 Aug 2026: US-origin pet dogs need the NWS (screwworm) inspection certificate above; a missing or incorrect certificate can mean the dog is refused entry or held at the border (USDA APHIS).
  • Returning to the US: APHIS has treated Mexico as screwworm-affected since 22 Nov 2024, so every dog entering the US from Mexico, including US dogs coming home, needs a screwworm-freedom certificate: inspection no more than 5 days before travel, signed by an official vet or by a clinical vet authorised by SENASICA (APHIS rules; SENASICA list of authorised vets).
  • At the OISA office the officer checks for signs of disease, external parasites and fresh or healing wounds. If ticks are found, the pet is held until the lab confirms them, at the owner’s cost.
  • The carrier must be clean: bedding, puppy pads, newspaper, toys and food are removed and destroyed; only the food ration for the day of arrival is allowed.
  • Frequent travellers can ask SENASICA to join the ‘Pet Program – Frequent Traveler’.
  • Pet ferrets from the US are currently refused because of screwworm; pet rabbits from the US have been refused since 23 Apr 2010.

Cats

Rabies
From US/CA: not required at entry.
Health certificate
From US: none since 16 Dec 2019; no NWS doc for cats.
Microchip
Not required.
  • Do NOT add cats to the dog NWS form — invalidates it (APHIS).

Return to the United States

CDC sets the rules for every dog entering the US: at least 6 months old, microchipped, healthy, and with a receipt from the online CDC Dog Import Form. Mexico is not on CDC’s high-risk list for dog rabies, but check the CDC dog import page if your dog has been elsewhere in the past 6 months. Dogs coming from Mexico also need the screwworm certificate above. Cats have no CDC import form.

Grooming

Estética canina culture is strong; chains (Petco) require the Mexican 'cartilla de vacunación'. Mobile 'a domicilio' services are common in CDMX and often skip vaccine papers.

Chain salons ask for 'cartilla' — bring the rabies certificate translated or use a mobile groomer.
