People taking a pet dog or a pet bird from the United States into Mexico. The inspection-document rule does not apply to pet cats. Ferrets from all U.S. states are prohibited.

Since 3 August 2026, Mexico has required a New World screwworm inspection document for pet dogs and pet birds leaving the United States. A USDA-accredited veterinarian must complete and sign the dog certificate no more than five days before entry; APHIS does not endorse it. Border inspectors also check that the dog appears healthy, has no fresh or healing wounds, and is free of external parasites. Missing or incomplete paperwork can lead to refusal or detention. Pet cats are not covered and are inspected on arrival without this certificate. Pet bird travel was allowed to start again on that date, with a separate addendum and an endorsed health certificate. Ferrets from all U.S. states remain prohibited.

The official USDA APHIS page does not state a separate announcement date. The page was last modified on 27 October 2026. The requirement is effective 3 August 2026.
