Official pet-travel rule updates from CDC, USDA, airlines and destination authorities, each with a dated official source.

    UK and EU target 1 March 2028 for SPS agreement and multiuse pet passports

    At the 20 November 2026 EU–UK Summit, the UK and EU welcomed the conclusion of negotiations on a Common Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) Area and stated they aim to apply the agreement from 1 March 2028, subject to UK and EU procedures. DEFRA states that pet owners and assistance dog users travelling from Great Britain to the EU will then be able to get a multiuse pet passport instead of a new Animal Health Certificate for each trip. Multiuse pet passports are not available yet; until then, an Animal Health Certificate remains required.

  • 20Nov2026
    Countries & quarantineOfficial source

    Mexico requires New World screwworm inspection documents for pet dogs and pet birds from the United States

    Since 3 August 2026, Mexico has required a New World screwworm inspection document for pet dogs and pet birds leaving the United States. A USDA-accredited veterinarian must complete and sign the dog certificate no more than five days before entry; APHIS does not endorse it. Border inspectors also check that the dog appears healthy, has no fresh or healing wounds, and is free of external parasites. Missing or incomplete paperwork can lead to refusal or detention. Pet cats are not covered and are inspected on arrival without this certificate. Pet bird travel was allowed to start again on that date, with a separate addendum and an endorsed health certificate. Ferrets from all U.S. states remain prohibited.

  • 20Nov2026
    Countries & quarantineOfficial source

    Japan sets a 14-day export inspection deadline for dogs and rabbits from 1 January 2027

    From 1 January 2027, people taking a dog or a rabbit out of Japan must apply to the Animal Quarantine Service for export inspection at least 14 days before departure. The change comes from an amendment to the rules that implement Japan’s law on domestic animal infectious diseases. Slots for dogs, cats and rabbits are limited, so the service advises applying earlier than the minimum. A second rule starts the same day: rabbits arriving in Japan as cargo must be reported to AQS a minimum of 40 days ahead of import. Owners bringing a rabbit as accompanied baggage are encouraged to give notice as early as they can, because quarantine space depends on what is available.

  • 20Nov2026
    Countries & quarantineOfficial source

    New Animal Health Certificate template required for Great Britain pet travel from 1 October 2026

    A new Animal Health Certificate form, introduced when European Union pet travel rules changed on 22 April 2026, became the required version for official veterinarians in Great Britain on 1 October 2026. The previous form could still be issued through 30 September 2026, under a transition agreed by the EU. From 1 October 2026, veterinarians must issue the new form. Certificates issued in Great Britain are accepted for dogs, cats and ferrets returning from the EU or other part 1 listed countries. Each certificate remains valid for six months from the issue date, or until the animal’s rabies vaccination expires, whichever is earlier. Official guidance notes that both versions may still be in circulation for a time.
