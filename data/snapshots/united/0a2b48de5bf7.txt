Traveling with pets
Bringing your cat or dog with you on your trip is fun, but there are some rules you’ll have to follow. If you’re looking for information on traveling with service animals , we can also help with that.
We no longer let you check your pets through the PetSafe program. Pets can only fly in cargo when traveling with certain active-duty military or State Department employees. If you plan to fly with your pet, they must be a cat or dog and stay in the cabin with you ( if there’s space available ). Tickets cost $150 each way.
On most flights, you can bring up to 2 pets per person. You must buy 2 seats if you’re bringing 2 pets. The second seat needs to be next to your assigned seat, so you can care for both pets.

Going to the U.S. with a dog
The United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) may require a certificate if your pet dog is coming from a country or region where screwworm is known to exist. Many countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America are exposed to screwworm. More information on the screwworm requirements for your dog and areas that are affected can be found in the USDA website.
Going to Mexico with a dog
Customers traveling with dogs into Mexico will be required to present a New World Screwworm (NWS) Inspection certificate. This applies to service dogs and those traveling as in-cabin pets.
To receive the NWS Inspection certificate, the dog must:
Be free of internal and external parasites, as confirmed by a health certificate.
Have a certificate signed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and issued no more than 5 days before travel.
Customers with dogs originally from Mexico don’t need an NWS Inspection certificate. They do need to have a health certificate and a vaccination record that was issued in Mexico. An NWS Inspection certificate isn’t needed for dogs that are flying through the U.S., but are from another country.
More information can be found on the USDA website .
For all international travel, make sure you're aware of the specific entry and exit requirements for your destination. United Airlines requires that dogs and cats have a health and vaccination certificate for all international flights, including flights to Canada and Mexico.
Where can I fly with my pet?
As long as your destination allows pets, you can take them with you on any flight operated by United or United Express. If you’re flying internationally, you’ll need to call to add a pet to your reservation. If you’re traveling on multiple airlines, including our partners, you’ll also need to check with each one to see what their pet policy is. If you need further assistance, click the "Let's Chat" icon below.

Make sure your pet fits
While there are no weight or breed limitations for pets, they must travel in either a hard-sided or soft-sided carrier. The only animals allowed on planes without a pet carrier are service animals .
Hard-sided carrier
The maximum size of a hard-sided pet carrier can be 9 in x 17.5 in x 12 in (22 cm x 44 cm x 30 cm).
Hard-sided carrier
The maximum size of a hard-sided pet carrier can be 9 in x 17.5 in x 12 in (22 cm x 44 cm x 30 cm).
Soft-sided carrier
The maximum size of a soft-sided pet carrier can be 11 in x 18 in x 11 in (27 cm x 45 cm x 27 cm).
Soft-sided carrier
The maximum size of a soft-sided pet carrier can be 11 in x 18 in x 11 in (27 cm x 45 cm x 27 cm).
Some countries don’t allow pets to travel at all. Others have strict rules for rabies vaccines . Make sure you have all your paperwork and proof of vaccination in order before you leave.
Booking pet tickets
Pet tickets are only for cats or dogs and cost $150 each way. All you have to do is select “Travel with a pet” as a traveler during booking on united.com. Or, you can go to My Trips to add a pet to an existing trip. You cannot add a pet to your trip on the United app.

Pet relief areas
Many airports have places you can take your pet to use the bathroom while you’re there. Use the airport maps feature on the United app to see where they’re located.
Please note, therapy and emotional support cats and dogs must fly as pets, not service animals. They must follow all rules outlined in this policy both at the airport and on the plane.

Traveling with pets FAQs
We require rabies and health certificates for dogs and cats traveling from the United States to all international locations. The rabies vaccinations must be completed at least 28 days before travel for both dogs and cats. Any additional rules for traveling to the U.S. depend on whether or not you’re traveling from a country the CDC considers to be high risk for rabies. We closely follow CDC guidelines on these specific country requirements and recommend checking them to make sure you meet them at least two months before your trip.
If you’re traveling within the U.S. with your pet, we encourage you to get a certificate of health from your vet. Some destinations may request this certificate on arrival. It should include:
Info about your pet, including breed, sex, age, color and markings
Where can I sit with my pet on the plane?
Where you can sit with your pet depends on the type of plane you’re on. Keep in mind that while you can only bring one pet with you, each plane limits the total number of pets allowed on the same flight.

Do you have military or State Department pet exemptions?
We do have pet exemptions for members of the U.S. military on permanent change of station and their spouses, as well as State Department foreign service officers and their spouses.
How old do my pets have to be to fly?
On domestic flights, puppies and kittens must be at least 2 months old. On international flights, dogs must be at least 6 months old to comply with CDC requirements. Kittens need to be 4 months old to comply with vaccination requirements.
